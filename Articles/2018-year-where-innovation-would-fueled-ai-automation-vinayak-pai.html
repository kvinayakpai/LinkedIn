2018: Year where innovation would be fueled by AI and Automation

Created on 2017-12-24 05:27

Published on 2017-12-24 07:37

Innovation fueled by AI and Automation, would be the recurring theme across boardrooms in 2018

Let me share with you as to why I think along these lines.

AI is going to be the most used word in 2018. We are already getting a glimpse of this. Every organization wants its name to be associated with AI. Everyone wants to say that they are doing AI or their product has inbuilt capabilities of AI. So let us see what really is AI and what do they mean by it.

Image Source: https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/artificial-intelligence-an-introduction/

The above image is one of the most used pictures to define what is AI. I would not define it, but let you explore the source of the image and other articles to find the scholastic definition of AI. But let me share with you where you are seeing AI already and where all you could be seeing AI in the near future. When you see SPAM filters which classify emails automatically and move SPAM to junk folder, you are seeing AI in action. When you type a sentence in a word processing tool and it highlights a possible grammatical error, then you are seeing AI in action. If you are using the auto-suggest feature while typing text on your mobile device, then you are seeing AI in action. If you are using an assistant like Goole assistant or Siri, you are seeing AI in action. Pretty soon you would start conversing with in-animate objects (like Alexa) and they would start responding intelligently and the line between real and virtual would start blurring, pretty soon you would be comfortable being driven by a driverless vehicle, pretty soon you would learn how to work with an AI system, like you use your calculator or computer today.

All products that you would be using would offer some feature or the other which would make your experience with their product better and they would attribute it to AI embedded in their products.

Now let us see how most of what you would see as AI would be AI powered by Automation.

Image Source: https://www.ericnagel.com/motivation/automation-ftw.html

So what is automation? Automation is not a new concept, during industrial revolution, machines were used to automate a lot of manual work done by humans and during the age of computerization, computers were used to automate lot of manual work done by humans. 

Humans have always fought automation and then learnt to live with it.

I like this quote from Bernard Marr, as it summarizes automation in the age of computerization “before the invention of the ATM, there were around half a million bank tellers in the US. Today, we have about half a million bank tellers. So people are pretty good about finding other things to do when key tasks like dispensing cash and so on get taken over by machines. So my argument is that there will be some job losses but they will be marginal rather than dramatic, and the people who are good at working with machines will probably do fairly well at least for a while.”

Automation in the age of AI

Traditionally Automation is closely linked to Rule Engines like the good old excel macros. Today during the AI revolution, a lot of manual work done by humans (who are already using computer), which is considered mundane requiring basic intelligence is going to get automated. So what is basic intelligence?

Rule Engines have been used for a long time to automate a set of tasks, however when there was a bit of decision making involved, there were humans who were brought into be part of the process working with the rule engine.

For example., As part of a MIS report creation, an excel macro could connect to multiple data sources and pull data, a human then would connect to multiple SAP screens and enrich that data and create the final report. While he is connecting to the SAP screens, he would look at the data and make decisions whether to include the data as is or compare it with another source and modify accordingly. While this required human intelligence, the level of intelligence required was basic, looking at multiple variables, and taking a decision, something that can be taught to an AI. With a bit of Machine Learning, the whole decision making process is automated and the erstwhile Rule Engine, now becomes powered by AI.

This is how automation and AI are coming together. So most of what would get presented to you as AI would mostly be automation with a bit of Machine Learning, but you would have seen from the image above that Machine Learning is just a part of AI.

Innovation is the future, and would be fueled by AI and Automation

The question then is, how does this lead to Innovation? The most common line supporting any kind of automation is –

“This would free up the humans so that they can do something which only humans can do”.

So at the moment we think that only humans can innovate, so humans can focus on innovation or more specifically “Business Process re-engineering”.

Let us look at a bunch of analysts working for an insurance company, who take the decision of whether to settle claims or go for litigation, or a bunch of analysts working for a bank, trying to identify if a transaction is fraudulent or is it just a false alarm. In both the cases, the analysts would have decades of experience doing the work repeatedly and would have developed an approach to solve the problem. As Morgan Housel would say, they would have developed a simple rule of thumb. Now what if this approach or rule of thumb could be taught to a ML program?

As part of their job, they would need to go through a lot of legal documents and identify the relevant sections and take decisions based on that. What if an ML program could be taught to use NLP (Natural Language Processing) to read the documents and provide the relevant section to them? Or better still, based on the relevant sections, help them with a go-no-go decision?

The role of the Analyst then would be like a curator, as outlined in this interesting article by Sveta McShane. She describes a future role of a curator as one who looks at a list of computer generated options and chooses the right one (I could relate this to the auto-suggest feature on my android as I start typing a response to a message).

Going one step ahead, the job of the analyst then would be look at all the different aspects that a AI program considered before coming to the decision and approve it. (Reminds me of an appointment with a specialist at a hospital, the interns and junior staff collect all the information required, would take decisions and then place it in front of the specialist, and the specialist then communicates the decision to the patient, as Subroto writes about Dr. Devi Shetty in his book Professional)

In one of my earlier blogs, I had written about how Disruptive Innovation as explained by “Clayton M. Christensen”, would be a major force behind successful organizations of the future. When AI and Automation come together, they free up the time of specialists who could then focus on “Business Process re-engineering”. So what is “Business Process re-engineering”? I would go back to the story of how Rockefeller saved money by saving one drop of solder. When people who understand the business processes best, get to the details, identify the loopholes, identify where revenue is leaking, identify where there is scope for automation and AI to drive Business Process Improvement and thus make business more profitable and successful.

Thus, I think 2018 would be an year where innovation would be fueled by AI and Automation.
