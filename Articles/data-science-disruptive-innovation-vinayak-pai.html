Data Science and Disruptive Innovation

Created on 2017-04-24 07:12

Published on 2017-04-24 07:19

The theory of disruptive innovation introduced by Joseph L. Bower and Clayton M. Christensen in the 1995 HBR article is something which is at play behind the current popularity of or hype around Data Science. The gist of the theory is that “when established companies don’t change with changing times, there are new entrants into the market who bring in disruptions and often topple the leaders. “. In order to avoid this disruption, Industry leaders question the “Status Quo”.

When they question the status quo they are faced with the complex choice of stepping ahead amidst uncertainty and indecision. It is the followers who wait for a well-defined path, but the leaders define the path when there is none, analyze the options they have and choose a disruptive path. Something similar happened with the industry a decade back, Industry leaders chose to work on “Data Science” as it is popularly known today, came up with solutions to use the data their businesses were generating to predict the course their businesses would take. Not all the efforts were successful, as is the case with any innovative effort. When they found good results, the followers followed the trodden path. This is the reason why we are seeing the heightened interest in Data Science.
