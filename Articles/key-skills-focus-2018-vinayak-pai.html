Key skills to focus - in 2018

Created on 2017-11-11 17:28

Published on 2017-11-11 17:37

“I want to get into Big Data Analytics”, “I want to get into Data Science” , “How do I go about”, “What skills do I need to pick up”, “Where do I start from” – These are the questions that I get asked almost every week, by a different person. I have seen myself giving different answers to different people. I believe that the path is never going to be the same for all. I attempt to understand their strengths and try to build around it, rather than asking them to start from scratch on a skill which they don’t have.

 My own thinking around this has evolved over a period of time which can be seen in my earlier blogs Leap from Traditional DW/BI to Advanced Analytics - Part1, 6 Skills required for a Data Science Career, How to choose a data science course?, How to start a Data Science journey and List of Improvement Areas for a Data Scientist.

 In one of my previous blogs, I had written that “The world as we know is changing fast, if we don’t change fast, then we would be part of history faster!”

 

So my own advice to enthusiasts getting into data space has also changed!

 Here I attempt to list the skills which I believe one should focus on in 2018

  1.  Business Knowledge
  2. Programming Skills
  3. Cloud
  4. Mathematics & Statistics
 Business Knowledge

 Technology is never for the sake of technology, at least when it moves into mainstream from academics, and Business Value is the invisible power behind any hype that you see in the market. So how does one build business knowledge? Start with understanding the basics of the domain. Search for 101 courses on the domain like the Supply Chain courses at MIT, look up for KPI libraries to understand the terminologies, look at annual reports of the customers that you are working with, look at their competitors and understand the trends in the industry. One of the simple but effective methods that I have used is to create a “Google Alert” on the topic. It starts bringing in any fresh news on the topic almost on a daily basis. Another approach is to identify forums where a topic is discussed, enroll there and be a silent observer and learn. Yet another approach is to look at Industry research discussed in How to see ahead of the curve. To summarize, this is a never ending journey and if you haven’t started already, start this journey at least in 2018.

Programming Skills

When I started my career, my seniors told me, if you know one programming language and one database well, then you would be able to survive in the industry. This seems to hold good even today. I recently started reading about Spark and found that my database knowledge (of Oracle internals) helped me understand the “Explain Plan” and “Cost Based Optimizer” of Spark. My knowledge of SQL helped me understand how spark works while reading the book “The Definitive Guide to Apache Spark”. Like “Fashion trends” that repeat every few years, trends in IT also tend to repeat, first programming was important, then having a GUI was important, then having RAD (Rapid Application Development) tools was important and then a framework/platform and now we are back to coding again. So deep programming skills in one language never goes out of fashion. During my initial days, my manager asked everyone to learn Java, and now we see java everywhere again as the force behind the score of big data tools out there. To summarize, programming never goes out of fashion, so if you don’t know Java, start learning in 2018, then add Python, Scala, Julia and so on…

Cloud

In my previous blog, I discussed at length how the world is moving in the direction of Hybrid and one of the most important skills for 2018 would be cloud. It could be AWS or Azure or Google, I ask people to add it to their kitty. Cloud is going to be used for cheap storage, fast processing and ease of scaling. Movement to Public cloud is no more an option, but a mandate coming out of boardrooms.

Mathematics & Statistics

The next big wave that is going to hit us, or something which has already started hitting us in parts is AI. Currently it is the era of platforms which make AI as simple as an API call, but as discussed earlier, it would go back again to the basics, and knowledge of Mathematics and Statistics which is at the core of AI, will become a “hotter” skill

 

So in Summary, Business Knowledge, Programming Skills, Cloud and Mathematics and Statistics are going to be my favorite skills to have going into 2018.
