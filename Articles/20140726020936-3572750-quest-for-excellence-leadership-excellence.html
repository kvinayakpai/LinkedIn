I attended the “Bullet Proof Manager” workshop from Crestcom and went through a video on “Quest For Excellence” by LISA FORD.

 

The following blog is about what went through my mind. I believe I had started on this journey some time back, however it is a journey and not a destination, so the journey continues..

 

Lisa explains the third area which is Leadership Excellence. She speaks of the following strategies

 

1) Leaders lead by example – Lisa says that “So if you want people to be honest and supportive, service minded, then you need to be honest, supportive and service minded. So we can't expect people to be more than we are”. We cannot speak about Integrity and Honesty if we ourselves are not practicing it. It was pointed out by the facilitators of the learning program that people who are parents would be able to relate to this point better as we are continuously being observed by our children. Children learn from the way the parents talk and act, similarly a leader needs to be aware that he/she is being watched every moment and this becomes a heavy responsibility. This also reminded me of the story of monkeys on a tree. The story says that organizational hierarchies are like monkeys on a tree. The monkeys at the top see the smiling faces when they look down, however the monkeys at the bottom see only asses when they look up

(http://pandawhale.com/post/2876/when-top-level-guys-look-down-they-see-only-shit-when-bottom-level-guys-look-up-they-see-only-assholes).

It is up to us to ensure that they see our smiling faces and not our asses (or faults and mistakes)

 

Leaders also help forming habits in their people. There are some infectious habits that they pass on to their people and make a change. A manager who looks for a detailed, error free document would put the pressure on his people to ensure defect free documents. This also puts the pressure on the leader to ensure that his documents are defect free.

 

2) Listen – One of the most important traits for a leader. I learnt from a workshop that I attended that it is important to be an active listener. One of the tricks of an active listener is that he makes notes (physical or mental) during the conversation and uses points from that while he makes his point. It is very difficult to keep our minds concentrated on what we are listening to if we are not genuine about the listening. I practice this and this has given its dividends. Sometime back I was given the opportunity of presenting my project to the customer. I was to speak about my project for half an hour, however the speaker before me was a master in his skill and an excellent orator. He kept the audience captivated and overshot the time and all I got was 5 minutes. I had to make my mark in those 5 minutes. I had made my notes while the customer was speaking. I had a list of concerns that they were facing in their current system. Luckily my project was solving similar concerns for my customer. All I did in those 5 minutes was link his concerns to how my project was solving my customer’s problems. I linked each of the features of my projects to the notes that I had taken and quoted the customer often. They liked it very much and I heard later that they even told the Chairman of my company that they were impressed with my capability to relate to their business challenges. This would not have been possible without active listening. The trap that most of us fall into is that we are waiting to make our point once the other person has closed his/her mouth, so we do not really listen to others, one of the ways of overcoming this is to note down our thoughts and continue listening to others. One of the managers I had worked with had this reputation of not being a good listener and I learnt it the hard way what it feels when your manager does not listen to you. It pays to improve one’s listening skills.

 

3) Create the vision and dreams – Lisa says “Humans have two needs. We want to stand out and be a star and we also want to belong to something greater than ourselves. So what's the sense of purpose that you give those around you so that they can feel like they're a part of something greater than themselves?” - This also reminded me of the Power of Vision from the book 7 habits and Viktor Frankl (http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Frankl)

Victor Frankyl was in the Nazi concentration camps facing the worst of and he visualized himself narrating to the rest of the world, how he survived the camp. This vision kept him alive and he kept people around him including the soldiers alive

 

Few years back my manager got me to write down my vision for the year and in the process he helped me share it with others. The people that I am working with liked the vision and I realized after listening to Lisa as to why they liked it. They liked being part of something greater than themselves, a cause, a dream, and a vision.

 

A few years back I worked with a team that had trouble with defects in their work. Customer had reached a point where he could not tolerate any more defects. I shared a dream with the team that we could have a 90 day defect free period. I showed the vision that our work will be hailed and we will be looked up to as a model team. Over a period of time and after some initial failures the team made it well past that 90 day defect free period. In the process the team became a bonded unit that still remains as one in spite of the project ending and the team being dismantled.

 

4) Provide the energy and enthusiasm – Lisa says “I recommend when you have energy and enthusiasm, tell your face, show it, people need to see it so that it might be inspired in them. I mean people with energy and enthusiasm have something called personal power. They're the kind of people who can walk in a room and they just command attention.”. It is very important for leaders to have infectious energy and enthusiasm. As managers we measure people by the level of energy and enthusiasm they display and so do they. This is seen as an important leadership trait. I believe that people draw from your energy and enthusiasm and I have experienced that I feel drained at the end of a counseling session. People would have gotten into the room with low energy and would walk out with smiling faces. I draw my energy from those smiling faces

 

Lisa concludes by saying “The quest for excellence means focusing on personal excellence, service excellence and leadership excellence. When you have command of those three areas then you can inspire them in others on a daily basis. What matters is how good you are at leading your staff. Excellence is what counts as a managerial leader today. To be one of the winners, your actions count. You can't just know what works, you must put it into action. Excellence starts with an attitude and an outlook and then becomes reality by your actions.”

 

If there is one quote that I would use to summarize the learning from this program then it is “Excellent firms don’t believe in excellence – only in constant improvement and constant change” – Tom Peters.
