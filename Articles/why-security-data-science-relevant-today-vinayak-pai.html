Why is Security Data Science relevant today?

Created on 2016-07-08 16:30

Published on 2016-07-08 16:37

Last Month I had the opportunity of attending NASSCOM-DSCI-Best Practices Meet – 2016. NASSCOM is the premier organization that represents and sets the tone for public policy for the Indian software industry. Data Security Council of India (DSCI) is a premier industry body on data protection in India, setup by NASSCOM, committed to making the cyberspace safe, secure and trusted by establishing best practices, standards and initiatives in cyber security and privacy. This event focused on the contemporary evolution of security under a dominant theme ‘Security Data Science’.

As part of the Welcome Address, Rama Vedashree – CEO, DSCI spoke about how digital channels are redefining enterprises. She also mentioned the NASSCOM perspective 2025 report and added that Enterprise assets are going online worldwide and this is fueling the digital revolution.

I went through this report and read that Digital Innovators have disrupted existing models and have captured 20-25% incremental revenues in select sectors. It also talks about how 80% of new spend (from the global IT industry) in digital technologies like Saas, Mobile/Online, Big Data/Analytics and IoT. This is going to help the Indian IT Industry too and its revenues could reach US$350 billion by 2025 and 38% of this would be from Digital technologies, interestingly this is 5% of the global digital services.

In this backdrop, Rama Vedashree said that the new area of Security Data Science which focuses on Cybersecurity, is expected to touch $35 billion by 2025. In an interview given to ETCIO.com she says “the digital enterprise will have three core attributes - customer experience and online services around it, data or decision making through data and secured experience, which is the cyber security part. We believe that data and cybersecurity is where the sweet spot is, which will lead security and analytics to become big disciplines in near future. That's why we are talking about security being a compelling domain for data scientists; otherwise they typically have been looking at lot of problems around verticals like financial services, healthcare, genomics, drug discovery and pharma.”

It is evident that Security Data Science as a field has got a bright future and Data Scientists should definitely be interested in it. I will try to capture more about the learnings I had from this event in future blogs.
