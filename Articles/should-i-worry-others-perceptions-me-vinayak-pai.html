Should I worry about other's perceptions of me?

Created on 2016-08-27 15:37

Published on 2016-08-27 15:43

This blog attempts to understand why perceptions are important and how to understand perceptions and how can we work on correcting perceptions about us if we want to.

 Why knowing perceptions about you is important

 “Why should I care about what someone thinks of me, if I know what I am doing is right?” is a thought that I often encounter during mentoring sessions. Not that people are forthright about it, but when I use this as one of the questions to ponder upon I get a sense that this is one of the most thought of question, but seldom voiced openly. I have detailed this technique of making people think about these type of questions in another blog “Mentoring through vaccination”

Once I have got people to think of this question, then I start with my journey through perception to explain why it is important. Through 3 stories I explain three main lessons about perception

  1. Perception Matters
  2. Perception lasts
  3. Perception travels

Few years back when I was reading Subroto Bagchi’s Go Kiss the World, I got introduced to the first two and then I learnt the third through my own experience.

  1. Subroto writes of a time when he had received 360 degree feedback (which is anonymous in nature) that he was not cost conscious after he took up a new role, when he spoke to his mentor he got the answer “Perception is reality”. Then he was told that he should treat the feedback with seriousness and start communicating at every possible occasion that he was cost conscious and provide ample proof.

2. A year later his 360 degree feedback still had the same comment though not as often as earlier, and when he spoke to his mentor he got the answer “it takes a long time for perceptions to change”, so don’t worry about it, keep working on your communication 

(I might have got the sentences wrong as I am quoting from memory, however I believe I have retained the essence.)

3. The third story is something I experienced, few years back I had a new project manager reporting into me and after few months of working with him, a perception about him traveled from his earlier work location through someone who had worked with him to someone who I was working with to me. The perception was “Be careful about him, he is like a snake in the grass”. I was startled with such a strong perception and may be my behavior to the person was irrevocably altered. So in my opinion it does not matter if a person changes organizations, the perceptions always travel. We are living in a “Global Village” where someone always knows someone else directly or indirectly 

So 3 statements, 3 messages to prove why we need to take “perception” seriously. As professionals we work with humans and humans form perceptions, and since we get affected by what others think of us, it is important to know how to deal with perceptions.

 How can you find what are the perceptions about you

In my opinion there are two ways of knowing perceptions about oneself, first one is to seek feedback proactively so that one knows the perceptions that people have about him/her, and the second one is to let the feedback/perceptions come to you. If you chose the first one then you need to create environment conducive for open feedback. It is difficult to get people working with you to be able to provide candid feedback, but it is possible through building trust. If we demonstrate our seriousness through repeated requests and share actions taken on the feedback provided and demonstrate that we value feedback, over a period of time it is possible to make it happen. I believe that once people who work with us realize that we are open to feedback then they could share their feedback openly. (I have experienced it)

Organizations which have implemented 360 degree feedback mechanisms have provided their middle/senior management a powerful tool. I have been seeking 360 degree feedback for last few years and this is one of the most awaited exercises for me as anything that the teams have not communicated openly is bound to come out in the anonymous form.

Tools like “Johari Window” are also very important. For me it is one of the mentoring topics and I believe it helps me explain the need to enable transparent feedback. Feedback is the only way in which the “Blind spot” quadrant can be conquered.

When I explain during my mentoring sessions that it is important for a leader to have a larger "Arena" to an extent that the “Blind Spot” quadrant becomes a thin line (I believe humans are complex creatures and some amount of blind spot would always be there)

Now coming to the second way of knowing perceptions, it happens ever so often that people come to me crestfallen about some feedback that they have received which more often than not is a perception. I have explained one such experience in another blog named “What does one mean when they say you have an attitude?”. Interestingly this is a blog that has had over 1000 views spread out evenly over three years, which means lots of people keep discovering about “attitude” which is one of the most common perceptions.

What can you do once you know what are the perceptions

Few years back I got a feedback that “I was not Social”. I knew the source of feedback and the reason also. I was in US with family and chose to spend evenings and weekends with family and not with colleagues. I chose to ignore that perception and few years later it resurfaced in my feedback again. I recalled the suggestion Subroto had received from his mentor and started communicating to people that I was not a social creature and generally do not go to weddings, house warming functions or birthday parties. I haven’t heard that feedback in last few years now :)

On a serious note, while there are perceptions that you would want to work on, there could be perceptions you could chose to ignore or act differently. But then there are certain perceptions that one should definitely work on.

Few years back I got a feedback that “I was not approachable”. I knew the source of feedback and the reason also. It was a slip from my side as I had yelled at someone unnecessarily for no fault of his. I had to fix it, so I started communicating to everyone that I am approachable and started few initiatives to prove that I genuinely wanted to work on that perception. I haven’t heard that feedback in years now.

Few years back during a meeting, I saw one of my past managers note in his notepad “Perception is reality”. Later when I checked with him, he said he had just written what he heard the customer say during the meeting. While I had heard it too, I hadn’t paid that importance to the sentence, so I asked him why he had written it. He seemed to not agree with the statement and said something which is still fresh in my mind “There is a thin line between Perception Management and Boss Management”.

So while it is important to be aware of the perceptions and choose to work on them, it is also important that we do not give undue credit to the perception and get into perception/boss/relationship management.
