Few weeks back I was watching Brain Games on National Geographic Channel and Jason Silva was asking a group of people to guess the number of gum balls in a container, there were 20 people and the guesses were ranging between 100 and 11,980. Then Silva called a young chap he introduced as the “Expert” who took a calculator and got an average and pronounced his guess – 2447. He was just 22 away from the actual number. How did this happen? He attributed it to “Collective Wisdom”. I started reading more about collective wisdom and found a site talking about James Surowiecki, the author of “The Wisdom of Crowds”

 Few days back I was reading about Machine Learning algorithms and came across the concept of boosting and specifically AdaBoost. Boosting is a machine learning meta-algorithm for reducing bias in supervised learning. Boosting is an answer to the quest ”Can a set of weak learners create a single strong learner?”

A weak learner is defined to be a classifier which is only slightly correlated with the true classification (it can label examples better than random guessing). In contrast, a strong learner is a classifier that is arbitrarily well-correlated with the true classification.

 http://advanceddataanalytics.net/what-is/

 http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AdaBoost
