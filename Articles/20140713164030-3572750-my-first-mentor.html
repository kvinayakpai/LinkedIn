My First Mentor

Created on 2014-07-13 16:39

Published on 2014-07-13 16:40

My first Mentor in professional career was also my first Manager. I have tried to capture some of my memorable interactions with him in this blog.

 

I had completed my graduation in Biology and had joined NIIT for a course in IT named GNIIT. At the end of a 9 month fulltime course where I learnt a lot about IT right from basic operations of computers to topics like MS-DOS, Unix, Sybase, C++, VC++ and VJ++. Then I was sent to placement interviews and I had the audacity to decline two opportunities I got (based on inputs from my mentor at home, my elder brother). This was my third placement opportunity and the pressure was on me to take it up.

 

His name was (and is) Steven Purewal and he was known to be a very strict person. So I made up my mind to be a tough cookie. We were supposed to reach there at 9:00 AM and were warned that we had to be there at 9:00 or not be there at all. The security person opened the gate at 5 minutes to 9 and let people in and closed and locked the gate by 9:00 AM. After two round of aptitude tests and a few hours, I found myself in front of this seemingly tough man who looked more like a Brit as he was born and bought up there but was a Jat Sardar by lineage.

Steve was one intimidating personality (later he would tell me that he was an Alpha-Male) and he went through my aptitude papers and asked some questions and then we had a very interesting conversation which I remember till date :)

Steve – So the only thing I see in common between your academic qualification (BSc – CBZ) and this field (Computers) is the C (of Chemistry and Computers). Why did you get in to this field

Me- Opportunity came knocking at my door steps while I was waiting for my PG entrance results and I took it

Steve – So you are an opportunist

Me – Yes, I believe that a person who does not take up an opportunity that comes his way is a fool (I knew what he was hinting at and he knew what I was talking about)

Steve – You seem to be very confident (of your capability to get another job and jump)

Me – Yes, look in to my eyes, can’t you see the confidence?

(I can visualize myself even today with two of my own fingers pointing at my eyes while saying that. He looked in to my eyes for a split second and the decision was made)

Steve – Do you have any questions

Me – You seem to be very particular about time, what has made you so time conscious

Steve – I was a consultant with Oracle in England, and every minute of my time was charged to the customer, so every minute was valuable

 

This was my first learning from my first manager and mentor

1) Time is everything, if you manage your time well then you can manage anything.

 

Few weeks later I found myself standing in front of him as I had not cleared the first exam of my training days (SQL). The rule there was that two back-to-back failures and one would be fired. Here I was with one failure already and a most likely second failure staring me in the face, so I found myself in his cabin.

 

Steve – Do you know the rules

Me – Yes

Steve – Confidence is not everything, you need to put effort to prove yourself

Me – Yes

Steve – If you do not clear the next one, you know where you would be?

Me – Yes

 

He spent only few seconds with me and asked me just three questions, however the message was delivered.

 

2) The lesson – Confidence can open doors for you but to sustain what you have gained, you need to prove your capabilities

 

That was a turning point for me and needless to say, I slogged for the next few days and ensured that I cleared the second exam (PL/SQL) in first attempt.

 

I completed the training and my first salary increment followed with a project allocation, however I found myself in his room again after few months

 

Me – Steve, I am very unhappy that my batch-mates have got an increment and I did not get mine

Steve – I think you do not deserve it. Not yet

Me – There seems to a problem here, I think I deserve it and you don’t think I deserve it. Give me a chance to prove myself and I will prove you wrong

Steve – What do you want me to do?

Me – Give me something which you think I cannot do and I will do it, however if I clear the hurdle then I think I would deserve a double increment

Steve – Do you know what you are asking for?

Me – Yes, I am asking you to give me some challenging task that you think I cannot do and I would want to prove you wrong (I know that you are going to give me an Everest to climb and climb I will)

Steve – It is not going to be easy

Me – There is no fun in doing things that are easy

Steve – This is the confidence that I hired you for (I hope you remember the lesson about confidence)

 

Few days later we met again and he asked me to clear OCP(Oracle Certified Professional) DBA certifications. He provided me the time and resources (I had two computers at my disposal where I could install the software and work, I also had the best books on the topic at my disposal. He also paid the exam fees and most importantly he gave me time – he moved me out of a project role, he paid me for preparing ). The next few weeks went in to preparation for the exam and I cleared the first paper (SQL & PL/SQL) as part of the certification series and found myself in his cabin again

 

Me – Steve I got 71% in the first paper

Steve – What was the passing mark?

Me – 70%

Steve – You barely cleared it, what happened?

Me – I was concentrating on DBA skills which is the next paper, this paper I felt would be a piece of cake

Steve – Fine. I would want you to score more than 80% in the DBA paper

 

3) Lesson - Confidence is good, but over-confidence is bad

 

I continued my preparations and attempted the second paper and there I was with him again

 

Me – Steve I got 81% in the DBA paper

Steve – How much did I ask you to score?

Me – More than 80%

Steve – You barely cleared it again, what next?

Me – There are three more papers left and I will take up networking, “backup and recovery” and “Performance Tuning” in that order

Steve – Why that order?

Me – Performance Tuning is the toughest and I want to take it up last

Steve – I want you to take it up next and score more than 85%

Me – (Silence)

Steve – Know that if you do not get 85% you are going to lose your job

Me – Yes, if I get it then I will get my double increment

Steve – (Smile)

 

Lesson – Face your fears and do not postpone the inevitable

 

I continued my preparation and this time there were my colleagues who were openly backing me and supporting me all along. Then there was one colleague who got his entire Church community to pray for me :)

 

I was in his cabin after few weeks

 

Me – Steve I got 87% in the Performance Tuning paper

Steve – Congratulations, now you deserve the double increment

Me – What about the remaining two papers?

Steve – You have proved yourself, so you don’t need to take it up

 

4) Lessons –

Higher the bar, higher you will raise

Never think that pressure (applied by a true mentor) is to break you, it is pressure that converts coal in to a Diamond

 

Few months later Steve gave me opportunities to become the DBA, System Administrator, Building Administrator and de-facto head of the organization and the only thing I was not doing was signing people’s pay slips. I got complacent and comfortable with my job and may be started feeling that I was indispensable

 

There were a few failures and there I was standing in front of Steve, with few other colleagues standing around (and the entire floor listening to hear what Steve had to say)

 

Steve – how do you explain this

Me – Sorry Steve, this was a mistake

Steve – No, it was a blunder

Me - (Silence – because I knew that trying to defend one’s mistakes was fruitless, especially with Steve)

Steve – Do you know I can sack you here and now?

Me – (Silence – my sense of job security was shattered)

 

5) Lessons –

No one is indispensable

When you find yourself getting complacent, get out of that job/role/project, shake yourself, bring yourself to reality

(Which is what I did 3 years later thanks to this lesson – I was in a very nice customer/project environment where I was the right hand man for the customer manager and was nearing complacency, and I asked to be relieved out of that role/project)

 

My false sense of job security was shattered that day in July 2001, a time when the US recession was round the corner and market was slowing down. A month later I had one job offer (only one) and the part I liked most about that job offer was a three month notice period.

 

6) Lesson - I told myself that , there was no such thing as job security. If I cannot find a job in three months (the notice period) then I was not worth it :), this forced me to keep my blade sharp and ensure that I was always employable

 

I am still in touch with Steve and I write to him and he writes to me. Few years back when I was in a role where I was supporting a team of around 90 people, I wrote to him about my role and my accomplishments so far. Here is a interaction between us

 

7)

Steve - Hello Vinayak - Glad to hear you are doing well. Remember "attention to detail". Demand that from your team and don’t forget it yourself! Good luck with everything

(Attention to details was something which Steve demanded and forced us to imbibe)

Me - Thanks for replying. I will indeed take your advice :) Was there some specific reason for reminding me about it? Did I miss any details this time around :)

 

8)

Steve - No, no you didn’t miss anything - rather i was alluding to not forgetting to instill in your own team principals that have held you in good stead - delegation is compromised unless empowerment precedes it

 

Steve wrote to me early this year on the eve of new year and there was one message that I took from that mail exchange

 

9)

“and as they say if it doesn’t kill you it only makes you stronger.”

 

Keep fighting, keep your nose above water in times of difficulty, keep trying and you will emerge victorious again at all times. Failures are bound to be there in life, you are not a failure when you fall down, but when you refuse to get up to fight one more time.

 

I keep telling people that I learnt a lot in the first 3 years of my career. I also say that I did not learn that much in the subsequent 3 years or any three years after that.

The discipline that Steve taught us in all the work we do, the perfection and the high standards set have made me what I am today professionally.

Thank you Steve for being such a great mentor :)
