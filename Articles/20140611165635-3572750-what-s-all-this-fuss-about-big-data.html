In October 2010 Eric Schmidt who was Google’s CEO said "There was 5 exabytes of information created between the dawn of civilization through 2003," Schmidt said, "but that much information is now created every 2 days, and the pace is increasing...People aren't ready for the technology revolution that's going to happen to them."

 

If you google this, there are lots of debates around the validity of this statement, however I would not attempt to get into that debate.

 

I am reading a book named “The Human Face of Big Data” by Rick Smolan and Jennifer Erwitt and it introduced me to NYTE or New York Talk Exchange.

 

Carlo Ratti, director of the SENSEable City Lab at MIT, tasked his researchers with creating what they called New York Talk Exchange or NYTE. The project illustrated how different parts of the city are linked to very different portions of the planet. The size of the glow corresponds the amount of IP traffic.

 

Another example that I read in the book was about BabyCenter.com which claims that today one in three children born in US already have an online presence (usually in the form of a Sonogram) before they are born. That number grows to 92 percent by the time they are two.

http://www.nationmultimedia.com/new/2012/09/20/technology/images/30190772-04_big.jpg

 

The extreme could be MIT’s Deb Roy who recorded over 250,000 hours of video and audio of his baby son’s life – categorizing and analyzing nearly every word heard by and, eventually spoken by his son, providing an accurate map of how one learns to speak and reason.

 

According to IDC (http://chucksblog.emc.com/chucks_blog/2011/06/2011-idc-digital-universe-study-big-data-is-here-now-what.html), in 2011 we generated and consumed 1.8 Zettabytes of information as a society.

 

What would all this data translate into? What does it mean? A chart from Wikipedia helps us understand that it is billion terabytes which in turn is a billion kilobytes and those of us who had used 1.4 inch floppy disk can count how many such disks would be required to store that data J

 

IDC goes on to predict that we will be generating 35 Zettabytes per year by the end of the decade.

 

What is our contribution to this? Every swipe that we do at workplace, every check-in we do in Foursquare or Google or Facebook, every tweet that we write or every Facebook post that we make, every conversation or forward on WhatsApp, every call we make, every web page visit that we make, every time we use a mobile app to make a purchase we generate a digital trail or exhaust and contribute to the vast digital lake.

I am a biology graduate by qualification and human body and the internal complexities associated with it have always been of interest to me. The possibilities of understanding ourselves better have increased over a period of time and the entire human genome was decoded 15 years ago. Few years back when Steve jobs was diagnosed with cancer, the genes of his cancer tumor and of his healthy genes were sequenced at a cost of hundreds of thousands of dollars, today thanks to big processing power it costs just $299 and requires no operation to extract the genes, but just a sample of spit.

 

When scientists were able to get the image of a brain cell of a mice the medical world was thrilled. Nanoscopy may allow us ultimately to see the function of a synapse at the level of resolution where the fine events occur, which right now are too blurry for us to see with standard techniques says Jeffrey Lichtman, Professor of Molecular and Cellular Biology at Harvard.

 

http://www.dnalc.org/view/2061-Microscopes-nanoscopes-and-finer-resolution.html

http://www.nanowerk.com/news/newsid=24180.php

http://dms.hms.harvard.edu/neuroscience/fac/lichtman.php

 

To link this to big data, we need to know that a few MBs of data is generated through a MRI scan. If we were to get the image resolution improved, then we would need to use Light Microscopy which would increase the data generated significantly to the tune of petabytes per brain image. But for us to truly understand the connection between neurons, we would need to use an electron microscope and a full brain image of a single human brain would generate Zettabytes size files.

 

If we were to link this to what IDC predicted, 35 Zettabytes per year, we know how far off the mark they were.

 

Well we have read about the world of data and the humongous amount of data that we are generating today and we are capable of generating in the future.

 

What is Mindtree’s contribution to this field? Are we there yet?

 

To answer this question let me take you back to the Gartner Hype Cycle and point at an area named “Internet of Things”. If every digital object around us were to have a RFID tag and were to be connected with each other to give us great digital experience like “when we walk into the house, the lights would get switched on automatically, the electronic gadgets would come to life and the whole house starts buzzing and the food from freezer would get defreezed and the food in the microwave would start getting microwaved, and the television would start playing our favorite show which was recorded for us and the temperature control would identify your needs automatically and tune itself accordingly”. I am sure you would thinking that most of this is in the realm of reality and may become a common thing in a few years.

 

When all these gadgets need to communicate with each other, it requires a Low Energy protocol which can work with a button sized cell which doesn’t need recharging for years. Mindtree has developed this Bluetooth stack and in partnership with LSI or lapis semiconductor has helped launch ML7105-00x.

 

The ML7105 series from LAPIS Semiconductor is designed for a new range of devices to enable the Internet of Things - Smart watches, health & fitness wearables such as heart rate monitors, pedometers and sleep trackers.
