History of Data Science

Data Science as a discipline has been there for a long time though it wasn’t called so. In a paper named “The Future of Data Analysis”, written in 1962, John Tukey was the first to talk about “Data Analysis” as being a distinct discipline. Peter Naur in his 1974 paper on “Concise Survey of Computer Methods” first used the term “Data Science” and defined it as “The science of dealing with data….”

Today Data Science has reached center stage because technology has made it possible to store huge amounts of data and analyze them at a low cost. Businesses now want to use insights from this analysis to help them run their businesses better and make them profitable and the discipline of Data Science is key to it. So this is the reason why “Data Science” is the in-thing today. It has the potential to unlock business value.

Importance of Domain knowledge for Data Scientists

If we look at history of Data Science and why it is relevant now, it is evident that it is because of the interest generated in businesses. The potential of generating business value has helped move Data Science from the domain of academics to mainstream. Hence it becomes very important that we look at Data Science from a domain perspective. For example, we are looking at use cases like “improve efficiency of the supply chain”, “Provide seamless consumer experience across multiple channels (as today’s consumers are more Phy’gital), by providing appropriate suggestions at the appropriate times, In the airlines industry we are seeing attempts to provide a connected travel experience to the consumer from the time he decides on travel till he completes the travel, in airline reservation, hotel reservations, car bookings, sight-seeing, business events and even leisure events. So if we look at the Industry, Data Science is being used more than it was ever used in the last 50 years. This is what makes it exciting and this is why Domain Knowledge is a key skill for a Data Scientist.

 Data Analyst Vs. Data Scientist

Few years back I was working with a group of Data Analysts supporting a global money transfer major. We were helping them understand their customer better through various analysis like Loyalty Analysis, Agent profiling, Promotion analysis, pricing analysis and Customer life time value analysis. If you look at these they are mostly looking backwards in to history and analyzing past information using simple tools like SQL and Excel.

Today I am working with Data Scientists who are helping customers improve their forecast accuracy, identify products that could be sold at stores where they have never been sold before, helping them plan demand for a seasonal product like Ice Cream and provide cross sell recommendations to improve the overall shelf space at retailers and through that overall wallet share of the consumers using complex statistical algorithms and even Machine Learning techniques.

Simply put a Data Scientist does Data Analysis in the form of Exploratory Data Analysis and also does much more than that. So I could say that the activities which Data Analysts do is a subset of what Data Scientists do. Since Data Scientists come at higher value, Data Analysts are being used to support them. Gartner even identified a rising trend among organizations to have “Citizen Data Scientist” whose charter is to support the Data Scientist and said for every Data Scientist hired there would be 5 Citizen Data Scientists hired. So a Data Analyst is best suited to become a Citizen Data Scientist and then graduate into a Data Scientist.

 Data Science Products

Data Science is a vast area and the business uses are also high. It becomes very difficult for one person or one organization to cover the entire spectrum. So there are going to be point products or services focusing on one business problem. If I were to pick up one business domain, say Supply Chain, even that is vast and there could be scores of Data Science solutions to service this segment alone. So within this, we have chosen a small segment like Assortment Analytics. We provide recommendations to the Salesperson on the field as to what products should he be pushing to the outlets on his beat. We have a cloud based solution that takes data from the DMS, mashes it up with syndicated data from Nielson and demographic data pertaining to the store’s region. We then use this data to churn out millions of recommendations. Through this we have helped $40 million in incremental revenue thanks to smart cross-sell recommendations at the store Level, 17% increase in repeat purchase rate, 4.5 million out of stock recommendations and 26% increase in unique lines sold per store. So Data Science products are the in-thing and there are loads of startups coming up with point solutions for point problems.

 Why Security is a key attraction for Data Scientists

Organizations have grown and have put in methods to “Protect” their data, they are also using sophisticated tools to “Detect” intrusions and “Investigate” as to what caused the intrusion. I feel that organizations are not doing enough to tackle the rising data security risks. Organizations either are not collecting and storing security data, or they are sitting on a pile of data and are not using it to improve data security. There are organizations which have started working on Security Data Science and have started using Machine Learning algorithms to identify patterns in intrusions and use that to better protect their networks or even prevent the imminent attack before it happens. Huge amount of data is being created in this space and topics like “Adversarial machine learning” are gaining importance. While Data Scientists can write ML algorithms to identify intrusion patterns and can even predict, adversaries who understand how these algorithms work, can devise ways to go undetected. So it like a game of Chess where the opponents start getting into the mind of each other, or like the mental game between “Sherlock Holmes and Professor Moriarty”

Future of Security Data Science

As per a study from “Association of National Advertisers” named “The Bot Baseline: Fraud in Digital Advertising” – “Advertisers will lose $7.2 billion globally to bots in 2016”. The prevalence of bots in the web world is a well-accepted fact, however only 12% of the websites have the ability to detect simple bots. The recent findings from the attack on Citrix is an indication of this. Social media and advertising fraud investigations firm Sadbottrue has discovered a botnet of three million Twitter accounts, which they suspect to be behind online services that sell or rent Twitter followers. Adversaries could already have advanced ML and DL programs finding ways to go under the carpet and not be seen by current or even future Security Data Scientists. So the future of Security Data Science is going to be an interesting space to watch as the tussle between the data protectors and data attackers goes to a new high.
