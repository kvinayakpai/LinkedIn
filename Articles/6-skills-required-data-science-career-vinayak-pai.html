Data Science as a discipline has been there for a long time though it wasn’t called so. In an article named “A Very Short History Of Data Science” published in the Forbes magazine in 2013, Gil pres has captured the journey of Data Science from John Tukey’s “The Future of Data Analysis” written in 1962 all the way to the present era. While Tukey was the first to talk about “Data Analysis” as being a distinct discipline, it was Peter Naur who in this 1974 paper on “Concise Survey of Computer Methods” first used the term “Data Science” and defined it as “The science of dealing with data, once they have been established, while the relation of the data to what they represent is delegated to other fields and sciences.”

In his Essay on 50 years of Data Science David Donoho from Stanford has captured the dilemma that Tueky and his successors had about the relationship between Data Analysis and Statistics. Donoho has even gone on to write “….the implicit message is that statistics is a part of what goes on in data science but not a very big part….”

So this is the reason why “Data Science” is the in-thing today. It has the potential to unlock business value.

 After looking at what are the differing views on Data Science and the skills required, as promised in the first paragraph, I would now attempt to share my view on what are the “6 Skills required for a Data Science Career”. But before that let us look at what are the key activities performed by a Data Scientist.

(I have already attempted to list the “8 crucial steps in the Life Cycle of a Data Science Project” in another blog. I started with 7 and then added one more step

https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/7-crucial-steps-life-cycle-data-science-project-vinayak-pai)

As per me a Data Scientist does the following activities (these are the high level activities and not an exhaustive list)

It is very important that a Data Scientist understands and appreciates the business processes. Domain skills play a major role and businesses appreciate someone who they can converse in business language. A Data Scientist should be able to converse with business data, as if they were not as rows and columns but as if they were living beings telling their secrets to an ear willing to listen.

The success of any project depends on the “Business Impact” it has created and “Return on Investment” (RoI) it has generated. To ensure this a Data Scientist should be able to carve out a small but significant practical problem which can be solved in an agile sprint. The next step is communicate this back to business, it is like reverse KT where we playback our understanding of the problem back to business to ensure there are no communication gaps.

Post that the real action starts to prepare the data for analysis. It is a common rant from Data Scientists that they spend most of their time preparing data for analysis, some even call themselves Data Janitors. The next crucial step is to explore the data and extract whatever juice it has through Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA). By this time the Data Scientist would know how to go about linking the business problem to data and to the relevant Statistical Algorithms. This is an iterative process and is repeated multiple times until the desired result is obtained.

Now I would attempt touch a raw nerve, Is Statistics the most important part of Data Science? Arguably yes, it is the heart of the Data Science project, however there is much more to Data Science beyond Statistics. The following is the Statistics I have been quoting for quite some time and have been able to convince mostly everyone on this :)

90% of the business questions can be answered by pure data analysis – You would be shocked to hear this but most of the real world business problems can be solved by simple data analysis or EDA

90% of the Real Analytics is done using simple algorithms – For solving real world problems a Logistic Regression is still an important tool that a Data Scientist uses

90% of the work of a Data Scientist goes in data preparation – We already spoke of Data Janitors. When I was quoting 80% with my team, they corrected me and said, 90% of their work goes into data

99% of data scientists also work on data preparation – Contrary to popular belief that a Data Scientist only writes complex algorithms, they also work on data preparation. This helps them understand the data better and choose the right algorithm to use

1% are super niche, heavy profiles – I was speaking to a Data Scientist at IBM who works exclusively on Algorithms, but then he is also an expert in his field who has spent over 20 years working as DBA, Data Modeler, and Architect and has invested a whole lot on data and is now reaping the benefits of that investment. This does not mean he can’t work with data, it just means that his organization has got other folks who prepare data for him. Check my blog on Citizen Data Scientists. (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/you-think-cant-data-scientist-citizen-vinayak-pai)

Business doesn’t care if you use statistics, but they think you do – Interestingly Business thinks that they would need a highly qualified Data Scientist to solve all their problems. However they don’t really care if you use statistics, as long as you are able to answer their questions.

To conclude, let me attempt to list “6 Skills required for a Data Science Career”

1) Good Communication skills – It goes without saying that this is THE most important skills that a Data Scientist would need. I have attempted to list what I mean by communication skills in another blog

https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140612015755-3572750-communication-a-bunch-of-c-s

2) Learn how to learn - Know your learning style – As a Data Scientist or a Data Science enthusiast, you should be prepare to learn a lot of new technologies. This should have been evident by now based on all the visuals presented above. So it is not about learning, but about knowing what works best for you when you learn. If you know your learning style, then it is easy for you to pick the relevant content that would help learn something new.

I had attempted to list some relevant content in another blog

https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/email-data-science-enthusiast-vinayak-pai

http://www.learning-styles-online.com/overview/

3) Data/programming (Excel, DB, R, Python, ML, DL, Big Data skills, Web data skills, ……..) – If you start reading about Data Science on internet, you would find that you are expected to know a lot of skills that you would have read in the blog so far. The one deviation I have taken is to add Big Data Skills and Web Data skills because I believe today’s Data Science is also about being able to work with the New Data. There is no replacement to learning as newer data in the form of IoT data, Smart Dust data, Data Security data starts flowing in

4) Problem solving skills (link problem-algorithm-solution) – Most of the times it is not about knowing how many algorithms are there or what they can do, it is about identifying what works best for the current business problem and what solution is the most acceptable to the business. Problem solving skills play a major role in a Data Scientist’s life.

On a lighter note value of problem solving skills can be explained by this joke about an exceptionally skilled engineer who was called out of retirement to solve a problem with a huge machine, who examines the machine and then marks the faulty component with a chalk piece and on replacing that component the machine starts working. He then bills $50,000 for fixing the problem and on being asked for an itemized bill he said, “One chalk mark $1; Knowing where to put it $49,999”

5) Presentation/Visualization skills – All the hard work done goes down the drain if it is not presented in an impactful manner. While Communication skills mentioned above could help to certain extent, you still need powerful visuals to tell a story. Story telling is an art and it is an essential skill for a Data Scientist

6) Interpersonal skills – As a professional, one needs to work with all types of people and building good relationships with all the stakeholders is very important. So it would be wise to invest in a course that specifically focuses on interpersonal skills and coaches you how to work with people. Till he gets robotic assistants to carry out every order he gives, a Data Scientist should learn how to work with people and get the work done.

 

I have attempted to list down what I think is relevant, but please note that this is not exhaustive and there could be finer aspects which I would have missed documenting above. Happy learning :)
