Demystifying Supply Chain Analytics (for the uninitiated)

Created on 2016-07-02 12:06

Published on 2016-07-02 12:12

When explaining what is Predictive Analytics Gartner has called out four types of Analytics, namely, Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive and Prescriptive in the increasing order of value to the business and difficulty in achieving.

Let us look at what Descriptive Analytics means for Supply Chain. When we analyze a Sales report by region or try to understand the customer behavior by segmenting them based on the revenue they are generating, or analyze the KPIs like OTIF (On Time In Full) to understand the on time delivery and order fulfillment rate or try to understand the current inventory and inventory movement (shipment) situation across the supply chain, we are doing descriptive analytics. Here we are trying to understand “What” happened in the past with the help of data. We are using data to describe what happened. Hence it is descriptive analytics. Here we use Reports, Dashboards and Alerts to understand “What” happened.

Now let us look at what Diagnostic Analytics means to Supply Chain. When we create reports as part of Descriptive Analytics, we often wonder, what is causing a product to sell more in rural areas compared to urban or why the sales of a product suddenly dipped in a particular market. Diagnostic Analytics, helps us understand the “Why”. Other examples could be trying to understand what is causing a particular service drop situation or why the customer service in a region has gone down. So while simple reports allow us to understand “What” happened using Descriptive Analytics, multi-dimensional modeling tools allow us to do deep analysis of “Why” an event happened.

While most of the Supply Chain players are already doing Descriptive and Diagnostic Analytics, Organizations are using Predictive Analytics to foresee a potential stock out situation and ensure that supply reaches before the event happens. They are using Predictive Analytics to sell products which they have never sold at an outlet or identify which SKUs have the highest propensity to sell at a store based on multiple variables. They are looking at data from their past trade promotions to identify what would work for future promotions. They are using advanced Predictive Analytics algorithms to match store and product profiles to see which stores have the propensity to sell which products. For Predicative Analytics statistical algorithms are used extensively. It is interesting to note that while there are scores of algorithms, there are a select few like Logistic Regression, K-means clustering, Decision tree and collaborative filtering which are used extensively. When it comes to Forecasting demand in Supply chain, Exponential Smoothening, Holt-Winters and ARIMA are used. As mentioned earlier, only 30% of the organizations are using “Predictive Analytics” to find out “What will happen”.

Prescriptive Analytics is about “How can we make it happen”. While Predictive Analytics gives understanding of what drives customers to buy certain products shedding light on what products they want, prescriptive analytics helps optimize scheduling, production, and inventory to better deliver what customers want in the best way. Supply Chain leaders are using Prescriptive Analytics to identify what could happen in the future and optimize their processes to take corrective action. They are using Prescriptive Analytics for identifying a stock out situation that could happen in the future and prescribing corrective actions to avoid loss before it happens.

So organizations are using Business Analytics to drive their supply chain growth. In the era of Data Science Supply Chain Analytics is going to take new shapes and it would be interesting to watch this space.
