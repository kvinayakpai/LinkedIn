How to see ahead of the curve - Journey with Gartner Hype Cycle

In my previous blog I had written about my journey of seeing ahead of the curve using different sources. I had also mentioned that my personal favorite was hype cycle in general and hype cycle of emerging technologies in specific. In this blog, I would want to take you through my journey with this report from 2010 till date.

The best way to read this blog would be to have the hype cycle images in front of you so that you also can visualize what I am seeing. Here are the images 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015 and 2016.

It is also important to spend a few minutes to read this link to understand how to read the hype cycle.

Predictive Analytics

When I first saw the hype cycle in 2010, Predictive Analytics was already on the “Slope of Enlightenment” and as per the report, it was less than two years away from mainstream adoption. You could see that it did progress in 2011 and reach the “Plateau of Productivity” in 2012 report. I would have expected it to be off the report in 2013, however it was there in 2013 also and eventually got off the report in 2014. This was my first experience with hype cycle and I could almost see this happening around me. We were working with one of the leading companies in the industry (on Predictive Analytics) through the period and sometime in 2014, we could see other companies also following suit. So this made me believe that there was some sense in these predictions.

Big Data

While I was lucky to be introduced to the concept of “Big Data” in 2009/2010 as my organization was working for the world’s largest citizen identification program, it made its appearance in the hype cycle in 2012, already close to “Peak of Inflated expectations”. In 2013 it went closer to the top of the peak, in 2014 it started sliding down the “Trough of disillusionment” and disappeared in 2015. I wrote a blog in 2015 why it was no more a hype. So it is not necessary that the technologies need to follow the hype cycle through all steps, it could skip the components and reach mainstream faster than anticipated by the report.

Internet of Things

I think now it would be easier for you to navigate through the images and see the movement of IoT. It made entry in 2011 as part of “Technology trigger” section and the report said it would be mainstream in 5-10 years. However in 2012 it didn’t make expected progress and the report said it was more than 10 years to mainstream adoption. In 2013 it moved up into “Peak of Inflated expectations” and in 2014 it really was at the peak and stayed there in 2015 also. However when the 2016 report came out, IoT was missing. There was an interesting entry in 2015 which was “IoT Platform” and it is present even in 2016. As we saw with Big Data, IoT also disappeared from the report faster than expected, however is present in other forms like the “IoT Platform”. Internet of Things is all around us today and we know it is mainstream.

By now you would be comfortable reading the report and might have started drawing your own conclusions. There are also interesting entries like “Citizen Data Science” which made and entry in 2015 (and I wrote a blog about it) and then vanished in 2016. Gartner says that if an item disappears from the report, it does not mean it is unimportant, on the contrary it means that it has already reached mainstream. Another example could be “Data Science” which made an entry in 2014 and was missing in 2015. This just means it is mainstream and it is already being adopted as is evident from so many data science courses all around us. Machine Learning made entry in 2015 and is present in the report in 2016 also. I wouldn’t be surprised it wouldn’t be there in next year’s report as I hear a lot of organizations already talking about how it can be used to solve the still unsolved business problems.

To summarize Gartner Hype Cycle is an interesting report which gets published around this time of the year which gives a glimpse of technologies of the future and helps me see ahead of the curve. How about you?
