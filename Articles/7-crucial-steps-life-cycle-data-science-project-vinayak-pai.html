8 crucial steps in the Life Cycle of a Data Science Project

Created on 2016-06-12 15:26

Published on 2016-06-12 15:34

 

As Data Science is at the center stage of enterprise growth, it is important to understand what entails a Data Science project. Here is an attempt to simplify it into 7 steps.

Data Collection – In the early years of computing, organizations had lesser IT systems to capture business data. All the data was internal, was structured and mostly clean? As the capability of IT to capture complexities of business processes increased, the number of systems increased. As organizations grew with mergers and acquisitions multiple complex systems had to be integrated. As organizations grew into global entities, the local vs global IT systems had to be integrated. As company’s network grew, the number of organizations that they are doing business with grew and today organizations have a complex network of sources from which they need to extract data in-order to do gain business value out of their data landscape.

The article depicts the complexities in today’s organizational data sources. How the organizations go about getting all this data would define the success of a Data Science engagement.

Data Storage - Historically data was stored in-house by organizations. As their data volume grew, they started having huge servers and racks to store their data. They then developed Storage Area Networks and complex systems to keep it available, back-up and make it available in case of disasters. Over time they realized that they were spending too many resources on this and decided to outsource it to Data Centers where someone else would own the responsibility of storing and supporting that data. Initially these data centers were holding only their organizational data and as Data Center services matured, their data was co-located with other organizations. The advent of cloud computing, and the lure of paying for what you use is taking organizations onto moving their data from data centers into cloud. The financial equation of Capex vs. Opex is also forcing organizations to move to cloud. While the leaders have already started their journey, the followers will follow suite soon. Deciding on what data storage strategy to adopt is one of the crucial steps in the Data Science journey.

While organizations have started moving their enterprise data to the cloud, with the advent of technologies like Big Data, Internet of Things, Smart dust and Micro Data Centers, the data storage and computing landscape is fast evolving.

Data Tagging – When organizations were small and all the data they were dealing with was internal and was properly classified/identified and known to them. With the advent of ERP systems this was abstracted from them, as these systems would use proprietary data model and cryptic names to identify data. For e.g. it would require a data dictionary to identify all the key figures in a SAP system. If you have attempted deciphering it for building business KPIs, you would understand what I mean. Roll forward to the present age of “Data Lake” where all data, organizational and external could flow in and be stored using big data technologies, identifying and classifying them would be a complex task. During a discussion on this process last year, we were wondering if there could be an AI program which would continuously work on identifying all the content that is moving into the “Data Lake” and do the “Data Tagging” exercise, much like the diligent Wikipedia administrators who sieve through all data that is uploaded onto their huge encyclopedia. If you have been part of a Data Warehouse (DW) building process, you would know the importance of business metadata, the glossary of the data stored in the DW. If you have architected a data lineage tracker or a metadata manager, you would understand the importance of knowing where a piece of data came from and where-all it is used. If you had compliance requirements to show the source and lineage of all data, you would appreciate this. If you were doing an impact analysis of a change, this would be your savior. In a “Data Science” project, tagging all the data that came in and was consumed by various downstream systems and the ability to trace back a “number” to a source is a crucial feature.

Data Linking – I started my career as a data developer and learnt all about data based on the Schematic representations of data, namely ER diagrams. As an application developer it was important that I understood all the underlying data structures of a master detail report in the front end. It was important to be able to link data from multiple data structures to arrive at a meaningful data display. As I started working in Data Warehousing projects ER diagrams which were in 3NF got replaced with the dimensional models. When I got comfortable with the stars and snow-flakes and the data linkages, I got introduced to Data Vault (http://danlinstedt.com/solutions-2/data-vault-basics/) and was fortunate enough to learn directly from Dan Linstedt. Data linkages between the hubs, satellites and links gave an interesting edge over both 3NF and Stars and Snow-flakes. Then started the era of “Schema on Read” and I was totally at loss, where did my data linkages go? How can I get data from multiple sources? As the cloud cleared and I started learning about the NoSQL databases, learnt about the systems which allowed me to still write SQL over NoSQL. With a huge population of SQL developers (or should by call them SQL literates?) Industry is trying to find ways of having SQL over big data sources. Amidst all this, no one would deny the importance of being able to link multiple datasets and provide a way of analyzing data. Even the original Hadoop eco-system had Hive and HiveQL. Ability to link multiple data sources and ability to visualize them is an important aspect of a Data Science project. You couldn’t do data analysis without “Data Linking”, could you?

Data Analysis – John Tukey’s spoke about the importance of data analysis in his paper “The Future of Data Analysis” written in 1962. So the discipline is neither new nor completely conquered. Every business would have done some data analysis or the other since their inception. The question is, is Data Analysis in the age of “Data Science” different. Answer is both no and yes. No because John Tukey and the writers before and after him have written enough about data analysis and Data Science has evolved from that. Yes because data analysis in the age of big data is both different and complex. Few years ago, I had the opportunity of listening to “Werner Vogels” – the CTO of Amazon. When I asked him about his view on what next after “Big Data”, he had said, organizations are busy collecting data and storing it. There is no value of this exercise unless they start analyzing it. He had predicted layers of abstractions that would be built over the Big Data landscape which would simplify the process of data access and analysis. His words, hold true even today. I see organizations moving their data into the big data eco-system hoping that it would somehow solve all their problems while they are still relying on basic data analysis or Descriptive Analytics. While data analysis was and is simple, the sheer complexity of the data in an enterprise compounded by the volume, variety and the velocity at which it is being generated make data analysis complex.

Data Analysis in the era of Data Science is both simple and complex. Simple because we have done it for ages and complex because of the complexity of the businesses.

Visualization – All the data analysis does not meet the desired result unless we are able to show it in an understandable visual format and weave a story around it. There is a business problem that need to be solved and how you go about communicating your understanding of the business problem, how you dissect the data from various angles and how you visually depict the output plays a major role. Like Data Analysis, Visualization is also simple and complex. While simple visualizations like bar, stack and pie charts have been used effectively for ages, advent of technologies like D3JS have revolutionized visualizations. Like I mentioned above most organizations are still doing Descriptive Analytics and simple visualizations would meet their needs. Advance visualization tools like Tableau, QlikView and D3JS offer rich visualizations that could be used to depict data. Still the most important part of Visualization is the story telling ability of the presenter or designer, who could take the audience from one visualization to another to communicate a business problem and the patterns that are coming out of the data. In the era of “Data Science”, Visualization plays a crucial role due to the variety of data. While structured data could still be presented using basic visualizations and meet the needs (as is with most organizations today), advance visualization would be required for the variety of data that is being analyzed in a “Data Science” project.

Around a year and a half back, I read an interesting article which revolutionized my way of thinking of future of visualization

http://www.forbes.com/sites/stevenkotler/2014/10/07/unlocking-big-data-lessons-learned-from-the-god-particle

Steven Cotler talks about the data from CERN during the process of discovery of the God particle being converted to a Symphony using “Sonification”. He then talks about our auditory capabilities to identify patterns in sound and using that to visualize data better.

In the same article he talks about a research in progress, which is working on finding novel ways of analyzing huge streaming data. As part of the project the Stock Market data is being converted to a snowcapped landscape and “Instead of turning on the computer to check how your stocks are performing, you could instead don virtual reality goggles and ski the stock market.” Talk about using all your senses to visualize the data.

Another interesting story around Visualization is in the TED video (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RE4ce4mexrU) where Deb Roy talks about how a large repository of videos was used to identify how a child learns a word. Between 11-17 minutes of this video he shows some interesting visualizations and how TV content could be linked to Social media content to get the pulse of the nation, especially around a time when the President of United States is addressing the nation.

I believe the future of “Data Visualization” and the future of “Data Science” are closely linked and that Visualization plays a crucial role in the story telling and communicating the results of the data analysis.

 

Business Impact – The success of any project depends on the “Business Impact” it has created and “Return on Investment” (RoI). The same would apply to a Data Science project, with the complexity that is listed above, it is evident that a Data Science project would also cost a lot to the organization. While organizations would follow the “Agile Execution” approach and spend in small pockets, it is highly imperative that they would also expect RoI in these small pockets. Like with any new discipline (while data science is not new, Industry and businesses are treating it as new), organizations step forward gingerly and are ready to slow down at the first sign of failure. Due to this it becomes highly important to demonstrate a “Business Impact” and get repeat funding for future phases of the project.

The objective of this write-up was highlight the “7 crucial steps in the Life Cycle of a Data Science Project”. If you were to take an abstract view at the points above, you would wonder what is new in the whole process. If Data Science were a new discipline then the steps would have been different, the steps mentioned above seem relevant for any data project. So why then is all the fuss about “Data Science”?

If you were to look at the changing business ecosystem and the importance of the “Data Science” space for the success of any futuristic organization, they can’t take “Data Science” lightly. For their survival in the big bad world, they would need to adapt to changing business landscape. Businesses are more connected than ever and Data Science is the key to the future.

The right question – As of today organizations are investing heavily in bringing all the data together, which is what led to the big data hype, and now that we have left that behind, it is time to make sense of that data. The huge set of data at their disposal could be the key to a lot of their questions but where do they start? Few years back we were doing a consulting exercise for an Industry major to help them identify what are the different things they could do to improve their adherence to SLAs from 95% - 100%. We had used a 9 box grid of Business Priority vs. Implementation Complexity to help them in prioritizing solutions. Organizations could use tools like this to arrive at key business questions and choose practically implementable questions from this.

The Right Question is the 5th Step in the process after "Data Linking" and before "Data Analysis"
