Is there something called data privacy? Or anonymous data? Should we care?

Created on 2016-07-18 14:02

Published on 2016-07-18 14:07

Latanya Sweeney of Carnegie Mellon University wrote a paper named “Simple Demographics Often Identify People Uniquely”. She took US Census data and combined it with publicly available “anonymized” medical records and de-anonymized it and mailed medical history of Governor of Massachusetts to him, to highlight that the notion of anonymization is not entirely true.

That was in mid 1990s and by 2000 she showed that 87 percent of all Americans could be uniquely identified using only three bits of information: ZIP code, birthdate, and sex.

In the 2009 article - “Anonymized” data really isn’t—and here’s why not - Nate Anderson writes about the above case and says - If that doesn't sound scary, just think about your own secrets, large and small—those films you watched, those items you searched for, those pills you took, those forum posts you made. The power of reidentifiation brings them closer to public exposure every day. So, in a world where the PII concept is dying, how should we start thinking about data privacy and security?

He gives two more examples of data leaks at AOL and Netflix and then makes this statement

“For almost every person on earth, there is at least one fact about them stored in a computer database that an adversary could use to blackmail, discriminate against, harass, or steal the identity of him or her. I mean more than mere embarrassment or inconvenience; I mean legally cognizable harm”

After reading the above statements, I came across Arvind Narayanan, who was covered in the Wired.com article, where they talk about his business card, which has only two words apart from his name – “Google me”. I started reading about him and his work and I was almost convinced that there is no such thing as anonymous data :)

This raised a question is my mind, what happens to my data? I have a significant digital presence and I have been using Facebook, Twitter, LinkedIn, FourSquare and Whatsapp.

This got me reading further and I came across an interview with CIA’s CTO Gus Hunt where he says, “Technology in this world is moving faster than government or law can keep up,” he said. “It’s moving faster I would argue than you can keep up: You should be asking the question of what are your rights and who owns your data.”

So in conclusion, I feel that in today’s world, there is no such thing as Data Privacy. If I have a smartphone and if I have installed apps, without going pages of information and clicking “I Agree”, then I have given rights over my data to some unknown entity. Am I paranoid if I were to say “Somebody can know where you are because you carry a mobile device, even if it is turned off”?

So the next time you are posting something on social media, or when you are using a mobile app to order some service, or if you are using your laptop to browse, please know that you are being pried upon and that you don’t have any secrets. Should you care?
