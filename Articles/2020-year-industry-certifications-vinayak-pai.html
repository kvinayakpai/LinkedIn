Summary

As we get into 2020, the theme on my mind is that 2020 will be the year of Industry Certifications.

 Certifications being granted by industry leaders like Amazon (AWS), Microsoft (Azure), Google (GCP) and Databricks would have their value in gold.

 During my interactions with hundreds of professionals as part of recruitment exercise, I have found myself getting excited on seeing a profile with these logos and most of the times disheartened as the candidates have lacked in depth knowledge of the area.

 So while the certifications are important to grab the attention and get an interview call and get an attentive ear, it is also important to build depth of knowledge on the topic.

 While certifications are a way of demonstrating capability, in reality it is the preparation for the certifications that matters a lot. If one is trying to understand the topic in depth and then using certification to test their understanding, I believe they are the ones who gain the most.

 On the contrary, certifications can be cleared with exam dumps or cram approaches and those are the professionals that I mentioned a little earlier, who I get disappointed with.

 So go for certifications listed below, but focus more on preparation and building the capabilities and then these certifications will be the “icing on the cake”.

How to Prepare

In the past few months, I have been recommending colleagues who come to me for guidance to pick up one cloud skill (could be AWS) and spend money on building a portfolio on that cloud. I have been urging them to read Mindtree Tech Beacon and look at the plethora of AI services being offered by AWS like Comprehend, Rekognition and Textract. I have been telling them to pick up a dataset from Kaggle competitions, and attempt to solve them in their cloud portfolio. I would tell them that, if they were to pick up one problem statement per month, try to solve it on their AWS instance using the services I mentioned above, then in a year’s time they would have a portfolio of 12 solutions which would be their IP (and not their employer’s or customer’s IP).

 To reiterate, while certifications are a way of demonstrating capability, in reality it is the preparation for the certifications that matters a lot. If one is trying to understand the topic in depth and then using certification to test their understanding, I believe they are the ones who gain the most.

 Here are the steps

  1.  Become cloud literate - start learning to work in a cloud environment (AWS, Azure or GCP)
  2. Start learning a programming language - Python
  3. Look at problem statements from Kaggle, download the dataset, observe the teams in action, learn from their blogs
  4. Create your portfolio on cloud where you would solve these problems, your-way
  5. Get the certifications

While certifications would add value to your profile, the preparation would make it worthwhile.
