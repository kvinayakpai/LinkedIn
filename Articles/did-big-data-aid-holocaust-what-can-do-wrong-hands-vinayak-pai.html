Did Big Data aid the holocaust? What can it do in wrong hands?

Created on 2016-07-16 16:14

Published on 2016-07-16 16:20

Recently I attended a NASSCOM event where I was shocked to hear of the possibility of Big Data being used as an aid during holocaust. I learnt that the Nazi regime used Big Data to catalog Jews. I also learnt about a book named “IBM and the Holocaust”

This got me reading and I have provided the links below. Some excerpts from the links

  • Only after Jews were identified -- a massive and complex task that Hitler wanted done immediately -- could they be targeted for efficient asset confiscation, ghettoization, deportation, enslaved labor, and, ultimately, annihilation
  • It was a cross-tabulation and organizational challenge so monumental, it called for a computer. Of course, in the 1930s no computer existed.
  • But IBM's Hollerith punch card technology did exist. Aided by the company's custom-designed and constantly updated Hollerith systems, Hitler was able to automate his persecution of the Jews.
  • IBM subsidiaries trained the Nazi officers and their surrogates throughout Europe, set up branch offices and local dealerships throughout Nazi Europe staffed by a revolving door of IBM employees, and scoured paper mills to produce as many as 1.5 billion punch cards a year in Germany alone.
  • People and asset registration was only one of the many uses Nazi Germany found for high-speed data sorters.
  • IBM's Hollerith punch-card system stored any information, such as ethnic type, profession and residential location, in the rows and columns strategically punched.
  • The cards could then be counted and cross-tabulated at the rate of 24,000 cards per hour, yielding almost any permutation of data.
  • Adolf Hitler's Nazi Germany and its collaborators killed about six million Jews.
  • The victims included 1.5 million children and represented about two-thirds of the nine million Jews who had resided in Europe.
  • Some definitions of the Holocaust include the additional five million non-Jewish victims of Nazi mass murders, bringing the total to about 11 million.

 

I think the above lines are enough to send a chill down the spines of the sensitive people.

The question on my mind is, if Punch cards the predecessors of modern computers can be used to kill 11 Million (?) people, what if today’s computing power of Big Data is used for a similar exercise? Alfred Nobel who invented Dynamite, never expected to be called “Merchant of Death”. Any tool in the wrong hands can be dangerous, and Big Data or Data Science is no exception.
