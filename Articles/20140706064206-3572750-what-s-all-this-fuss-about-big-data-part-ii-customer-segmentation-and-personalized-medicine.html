As per Wikipedia -

Market segmentation is a marketing strategy that involves dividing a broad target market into subsets of consumers who have common needs and priorities, and then designing and implementing strategies to target them.

 

Through Market Segmentation or Customer Segmentation we are able to identify a cluster (bunch) of customers who have similar buying patterns, capabilities and behaviors. There are products which are developed keeping a particular customer segment in mind. The concept of Segmentation is based on the “Divide and Rule” principle. By segmenting its customer base an organization is able to provide personalized service to its customers which helps it in improving customer stickiness, improve their revenue from loyal customers, reduce customer attrition and in turn save on the millions lost due to the gap between demand prediction and actual sales.

 

We were working with a retail giant, helping them reduce this gap by basis points and save millions for the organization. If organizations know about their customer and can predict their behavior, they can do better demand planning and hence cut the gap between the plan vs. actuals which results in saving millions for them, which then could be used to better serve the customers.

 

As per Wikipedia -

Personalized medicine or PM is a medical model that proposes the customization of healthcare using molecular analysis - with medical decisions, practices, and/or products being tailored to the individual patient.

 

While reading the book “Human face of big data” ( http://humanfaceofbigdata.com/ or http://www.amazon.in/The-Human-Face-Big-Data/dp/1454908270), I came across this interesting article about Lauren Brenneman and Isaac who are suffering from Cystic fibrosis, a disease that causes the lungs to fill up with Mucus. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=acC1ilWLZYs&feature=kp. Lauren and Isaac wear a special vest each morning to help clear their lungs and Lauren states that she would have to spend as long as three hours a day doing just that.

 

I would not like to repeat what is written in the book or said in the video, as you can read about her and watch the video too, however there was something that was there in the article which was the genesis of this thought.

 

According to Francis Collins, director of the National Institutes of Health, “thousands of lifesaving drugs are sitting unused on drug companies’ shelves.”. He also says “difference between curing a disease and triggering a fatal reaction can be as small as a few genes hiding among 20,000 or so in a person’s DNA.

 

FDA approves only those drugs which are 100% safe and effective for almost anyone. Due to this, there are thousands of medicine which could be able to cure few people, however might turn fatal for others, which never find the light of the day.

 

Thanks to big processing power (unleashed by technological progress) gene sequencing costs just $299 and requires no operation to extract the genes, but just a sample of spit.

 

Megan Krause, from Penn State University in her article “Big Data, Big Help” writes about pharmacogenomics “pharmacology + genomics = pharmacogenomics” and states this wouldn’t have been possible without Big Data.

 

FDA states that, “Pharmacogenomics can play an important role in identifying responders and non-responders to medications, avoiding adverse events, and optimizing drug dose.”

 

Now, if we were able to segment the diseases using genomics, and if we were to capture the genomic data of people afflicted with these diseases, we would be able to identify the responders and non-responders to a particular medicine.

 

Imagine the direct and indirect benefits of this, to start with, Pharmaceutical companies would be able to release more drugs (personalized medicine) to the market and People with rare genomic diseases would be able to get those drugs which are sitting unused on drug companies’ shelves.

 

Many of us would not be aware of the reason behind the prohibitive cost of many generic life-saving medicine. Ask people who have family members going through treatment for some medical complexity. Sometimes the drugs could eat up a family’s lifetime savings.

 

The cost of research and failed products is borne by the successful products thus saving the Pharmaceutical company’s bottom line. If there are lesser failed products (due to personalized medicine), that would automatically bring down the cost of the successful products thus helping the general public.

 

The lessons learnt from Customer Segmentation could be used to make “Personalized Medicine” a success and help the humanity as a whole.

 

We could do a patient segmentation and treat each segment differently, producing personalized medicine for them, thus helping us serve that patient base successfully.

 

I would definitely like to highlight that at the core of this change would be “Big Data” and our capability to analyze that, and this would help us better understand “What's all this fuss about Big Data”
