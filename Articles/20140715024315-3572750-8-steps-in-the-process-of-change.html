8 steps in the process of Change

Created on 2014-07-15 02:42

Published on 2014-07-15 02:43

We all keep hearing that “Change is the only thing that is constant” and several other quotes about change. We all are being told by somebody at some point of time in our life that we need to change. It could be a behavior, it could be a habit, it could be simple things or it could be complex things.

 

If change is forced upon us we resist it and we might not change at all, however if we feel we need to change then we attempt to change.

 

The post is not about how to cope with change around us, but how to experience and enjoy change within us.

 

This post is about the process we go through when we want to change. Those of us who “want” to change could go through the change joyously and enjoy it.

 

I associate this with a quote that I had read sometime back “If an egg is broken from outside force... Life ends. If an egg is broken from inside force, life begins. Great things always begin from our inside...”

 

I believe change is also pretty much the same.

 

So let me share my own experience with change and the change process distilled so that I can share how I enjoyed change.

 

I had the habit of pronouncing “towards” as “ta- words”, (ta as in tumbler). One of my managers pointed this out and then the process began. I didn’t realize how and when I had picked up this pronunciation, however the moment this manager made the observation, I realized that I was pronouncing the word differently from others.

 

Realization of a particular habit is the first step. The instant reaction was denial, That was indeed the way of pronouncing it and everyone else was pronouncing it wrongly. Then I went around checking dictionaries and couldn’t find anything to support me.

 

Then I realized that I was wrong and I had to change, especially since I had to stand in front of this manager and make several presentations. So I moved to the next step of change, which is acceptance, with acceptance the process of “self-observation” starts. I keep telling people that humans are the only species that have the unique ability to observe themselves in action. So here I was, observing myself, whenever I was speaking, and “towards” being a common word, would come up several times during a day. So the observer in me would point this out to me split seconds after I had pronounced the word and I found myself saying “ta-words” and then saying, “sorry - towards”.

 

Then the observer became faster and caught me while speaking, so I found myself saying “ta…towards” and then it got better, before I were to utter “towards” mentally the pronunciation “ta-wards” would get uttered and then caught and only the correct “towards” would be heard to the listeners. So there was a split second delay in my speech whenever I had to utter “towards”.

 

The next step was utterance of “towards” flawlessly, however the observer would make an observation post the utterance and congratulate me, so I found myself losing my focus for a split second.

 

The next step was free-flow of speech even when “towards” had to be used, the observer would still observe and report to me, however I was more confident that the change was implemented.

 

Now I have reached a stage where when I am narrating this experience to people I have to consciously pronounce “ta-words” to show them how I used to pronounce it.

 

So if I were to distill my learning from the process of change, here it is

 

  1. Initial Realization – Often happens when someone else points this out
  2. Denial – Denying that we could be wrong and then finding reasons not to change
  3. Acceptance or self-realization – Accepting grudgingly that we are making the mistake and we have to change
  4. Self-Observation – Where we start observing ourselves consciously
  5. Instant-correction – Correcting ourselves after we have made the “mistake”
  6. Catching it in action – Correcting ourselves while we are making the “mistake”
  7. Checking if we have changed – Observing ourselves every time we have corrected the “mistake”
  8. Change completion – The checking process is still on, however over a period of time, it moves from the realm of conscious checking to sub-conscious checking

 

So change is not difficult after all, we need to go through the process of change joyously and it would be enjoyable :) especially when you are a story teller like me and can narrate the process to others :)
