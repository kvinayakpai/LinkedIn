How to see ahead of the curve - experiences from a professional's Data Science Journey

Created on 2016-09-13 11:52

Published on 2016-09-13 11:55

In a fast changing world, it is very important to keep ourselves up-to-date. While everyone understands this, the question is how do we do it?

Recently when I was asked this question, I found myself answering along the lines below

“It is important to know which are the sources that allow people to see ahead of the curve, the following are some of the important ones relevant from Data Science perspective”

1.      Gartner

2.      Forrester

3.      IDC

4.      IIA

5.      Forbes

6.      HBR

Once we create a Google and LinkedIn profile and start searching for these and allow them to build a history of our interests, they would then suggest articles from these sources which are of our interest. I have found that “Google Now” accuracy of predicting what article I would like to read is very good as I read a lot on my mobile (android) device.

While most of Gartner research is paid, some of it is free like “The Gartner Supply Chain Top 25 for 2016” which requires that you share your email address and become a member. Gartner publishes Magic Quadrants on tools, the tool vendors then republish it on their websites like “Magic Quadrant for Advanced Analytics Platforms”. These papers help us identify what are the features that are relevant from Industry perspective and do a comparative analysis of the tools and in the process learn and see ahead of the curve. My favorite Gartner paper though is its hype cycle on emerging trends, which gives me a sneak peak of what is ahead of the curve. While the report itself costs a lot, the following article gave me a glimpse of what it contains and got me reading on Blockchain seriously. While I have been hearing about it for some time, I wasn’t sure if it is related to Data Science so I hadn’t paid attention, further reading on the topic lead me to another article and then another. This is how Gartner can be helpful, pick up the key words from hype cycle and start reading about them. My learning from the above reports is the best practices that the supply chain leaders follow which allows me to speak to supply chain leaders confidently, the different players in the Advanced Analytics space which helps me keep myself up to date and the biggest trend called Blockchain and the concept of how  Blockchain and blood diamonds are related.

On similar grounds the Forrester Research also has reports like “The Forrester Wave™: Big Data Predictive Analytics Solutions, Q2 2015” which obviously cost. However a little more search leads us to a republished version. Similarly a little search can get more interesting articles like” Massive Machine-Learning Automation Is The Future Of Data Science” and further search could lead to a republished version which is free.  Then there are magazines which will have articles where people would read these reports and publish their point of view.  My learning from the above reports is IBM is ahead of SAS as per Forrester Wave, the latest trend in Machine Learning is automation of the work a Data Scientist is doing and real business benefits of IoT for the masses is few years away.

IDC also publishes reports like “IDC FutureScape: Worldwide Big Data and Analytics 2016 Predictions” and some search would give us the republished version. My learning from this is that while organizations are investing in Cloud based Big Data Analytics, it is going to be some time before they start realizing the value of those investments.

A visit to IIA site would lead us to the free copy of the report “ADVANCED ANALYTICS & BIG DATA ADOPTION REPORT 2016” and my learning from this report is where are organizations investing, what is the attitude of enterprises toward Analytics and Big Data. I was at a NASSCOM sponsored event recently where I learnt that MSMB (Micro Small & Medium Businesses in India) are moving in the digital direction and the adoption rate is going to move to 70% in the next three years from the current 12%. This report kind of confirmed that it is a worldwide phenomenon because there was a chart which explained how mid-market investments are following enterprise investment in the direction of Big Data Analytics.

I am an active LinkedIn member and I see a lot of links from Forbes being listed there. This is a site which corporates chose to publish in and I learnt how “Internet Of Things On Pace To Replace Mobile Phones As Most Connected Device In 2018”. It helped me understand how smartphones are on the rise and so is IoT. I am reading a book from Bernard Marr named Big Data in Practice and as you can see he has a series of articles in Forbes and his book has links to these articles.

When Harvard Business Review published this article “Data Scientist: The Sexiest Job of the 21st Century”, a whole lot of people started paying attention to Data Science and even today links from HBR keep getting published on LinkedIn indicating that this is the choice of corporates.

HBR lists the article on Disruptive innovation as one of the top articles of 2015 and I believe it is going to be a phrase which all of us would hear more often. Few years back my organization gave me the privilege of attending a course from Clayton M. Christensen on the same topic. Till that point in time, I was thinking that Innovation is not my cup of tea and this course changed my thinking about innovation, hope reading this article would change yours :)

I would encourage you to share similar links which you use to keep yourself ahead of the curve!
