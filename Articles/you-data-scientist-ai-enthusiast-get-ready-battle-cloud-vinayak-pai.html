If you are a Data Scientist or an AI enthusiast – Get ready for the battle in the cloud in 2021

Created on 2020-12-29 16:10

Published on 2020-12-29 16:22

Summary –Data Scientists are going to become redundant, unless they align themselves to the winds of change. Year 2021 would take the battle to the Cloud, and everyone interested in this topic would need to know about all those cloud services which can speed up their work. They need to look at “out of the box models” offered by vendors like AWS, Azure, GCP, IBM, Salesforce, SAP, H2O.ai, Aible and a host of other vendors. They need to know how to use these models and then customize and scale them for production. They need to learn how to stich solutions using “out of the box models” and opensource packages. They need to know how to work with MLOps and all this would be on the cloud!


A decade ago when I started my journey in Data Science, it used to be called Predictive Analytics. There were three roles identified, Statistical Analyst, Data Analyst, and Business Analyst. Few years ago, when the term Data Scientist got coined and everyone gave their interpretation of Data Science and Data Scientists, I did my bit too 😊

My blogs in those days around 8 crucial steps in the Life Cycle of a Data Science Project and 6 Skills required for a Data Science Career , got 1000+ views and I started feeling I knew about this space. I saw people trying to get into the space and I felt there was a mad rush, so I felt obliged to write about How to start a Data Science journey. I then went on to write Why you shouldn't spend money on a Data Science course. But who was I to stop people from doing that? Month after month, year after I year, I hear stories of people who spend thousands (of rupees) to make a space for themselves and end up wasting their energy.

I tried to tell them that it is not about the money they spend on the course, but the time they spend practicing what they have learnt by solving real world data problems using read world data on sites like Kaggle and Analytics Vidhya.

On the other hand, there are people who somehow managed to get into Data Science projects and started working on solving one business problem or the other, and started feeling good. The world kept changing and now I am worried that many of these people will loose out, become redundant if they don’t align themselves to the changing winds.

Last year I wrote how Industry certifications are important and how 2020 is The year of Industry Certifications. I also hinted that enthusiasts and Data Scientists had to become cloud literate and start working on cloud.

Data Scientists of today need to know the cloud and for the next few lines I would use Gartner 2020 Magic Quadrant for Cloud AI Developer Services to substantiate these points. This report puts AWS, Microsoft and Google in the leader’s quadrant. A Data Scientist colleague of mine Varun Nambiar, did a research and evaluated over 50 AI use cases and how the services from these three top public cloud providers could solve them.

The Gartner report lists many players and I have just listed 4 apart from the clear leaders mentioned above.

  • ·      Aible speaking about AutoML focused on business impact
  • ·      H2O.ai speaking about “recipes” of AI design patterns
  • ·      Salesforce speaking about putting AI in the hands of every CRM and front-office user
  • ·      SAP speaking about customizable models that can be used as a web service by calling simple Rest APIs

The report doesn’t feature Adobe Sensei or a host of other tools which have industry specific models built into their products.

The writing on the wall is clear, understand what these large players provide out-of-the box, learn how to implement these for client needs and learn how to customize.

In a nutshell, the data scientists of today need to learn how to work with tools and customize out of the box models. Their real value would be in understanding how these out of the box models work internally under the hood, and ability to customize it. It is just a passing wave as organizations get a choice to make buy vs build decisions.

The aspirants on the other hand need not learn Python and R and be under the false pretense that they could understand the Mathematical equations depicting the model. They could instead learn how to work with these tools.

The other trend that is clearly underway is the wave of MLOps, that drives how Machine Learning models become explainable, how they become reproduceable. One of the key challenges businesses face is that they need to understand the systems that help them make decisions, as they are answerable to the regulatory agencies. It is important that their decisions are explainable and reproduceable. So, they would need to keep the version of the model, the version of the data and the parameters used for getting the output that they used to arrive at a decision. They need MLOps. ML models need to be treated like any other piece of code that would need to be stored in source code repositories, moved from DEV to QA to PROD environments pretty much like any other piece of code, following rigorous automated testing. They would need to have a plan to deploy these modes as web services on containers that can be consumed by simple Rest APIs.

All this translates to is a need for ML Engineering, as articulated in this page “MLOps: Continuous delivery and automation pipelines in machine learning”.

Data Scientists need to be aware of all these developments and need to be prepared for this and further changes that would come in the future.

Enthusiasts trying to get in to the space should understand that in the Data Science / AI world, there is as less as 5-10% scope for core mathematical, statistical and algorithmical work and rest 90-95% is around AI or ML Engineering. The roles like AI Architect, ML Architect, Citizen Data Scientist (one who understands the business side of the world and knows bit of Data Science & AI), AI Engineer and ML Engineer and more importantly Data Engineer (with deep knowledge in SQL), are the roles to go after.

The Data Scientist role would be 5-10% of the overall project work and it would be taken by people with core specializations in Mathematics, Statistics & Econometrics. So If you don’t have these qualifications then go for the 90% roles and if you have these specializations then pick up the newer skills mentioned above.

In summary, If you are a Data Scientist with core data science skills, learn Cloud, out of the box models and MLOps. If you are a data science enthusiast, learn cloud based AI and become a Cloud AI Developer, Engineer or Architect.

It is imperative that both need to know how to work with cloud, how to work with out of the box models and MLOps.

So, cloud is the new battleground and if you don’t align then you loose.
