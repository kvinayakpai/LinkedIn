Data Scientist - A T shaped Professional

Created on 2014-11-11 04:17

Published on 2014-11-11 04:17

As per Wikipedia, Data scientists

  • Use the ability to find and interpret rich data sources
  • Manage large amounts of data
  • Overcome constraints around hardware, software, and bandwidth
  • Merge data sources
  • Ensure consistency of datasets
  • Create visualizations to aid in understanding data
  • Build mathematical models using the data
  • Present and communicate the data insights/findings to specialists and scientists in their team and if required to a non-expert audience.

As per http://tsummit2014.org/t T-shaped professionals are characterized by their deep disciplinary knowledge in at least one area, an understanding of systems, and their ability to function as “adaptive innovators” and cross the boundaries between disciplines.

So the question is, is the data scientist a T-shaped professional? I think yes

As per the definition two vertical bars of the "T" represent the disciplinary specialization and the deep understanding of one system. A data scientist needs to have a deep understanding of data and the technologies required for working with the data. He also needs to have a deep domain understanding and very good appreciation of the business processes specific to the data at hand. Both of these form the vertical bars of the T.

As per the definition the horizontal stroke represents the ability to collaborate across a variety of different disciplines. In today's connected world it is extremely important to be able to collaborate with various stakeholders. A data Scientist is expected to know a plethora of technologies, understand business processes which previously were not known to a single person, is expected to cater to an audience which is keen to see business benefits and not technical achievements. All this requires him to collaborate with different experts and stakeholders to

  • Identify the right business questions through interactions with various stakeholders
  • Identify the right data sources through interactions with various technical specialists
  • Apply the right business rules to the data based on though understanding of the data
  • Apply the right algorithms, understanding the capabilities and shortcomings of each
  • Keep bouncing of the results of the iterative process through interactive visual medium
  • Incorporate feedback from stakeholders to arrive at the right insights

Leonard-Barton (1995) provided the drawing of T-shaped skills

The need for T-shaped skills surfaces anywhere problem solving is required across different deep functional knowledge bases or at the juncture of such deep knowledge with an application area.

People possessing these skills are able to shape their knowledge to fit the problem at hand rather than insist that their problems appear in a particular, recognizable form. Given their wide experience in applying functional knowledge, they are capable of convergent, synergistic thinking.

Industry refers to these people as T-shaped professionals, who are deep problem solvers in their home discipline but also capable of interacting with and understanding specialists from a wide range of disciplines and functional areas.

When I read the line “People possessing these skills are able to shape their knowledge to fit the problem at hand rather than insist that their problems appear in a particular, recognizable form.”, I remembered TRIZ http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRIZ

A Data Scientist would need to be able to look at a problem statement, abstract it to figure out if the same abstract problem has been solved by collective wisdom of humanity across industries and apply the solution to their problem at hand. So they need to shape their knowledge to fit the problem at hand.

While the blog mentioned above talks of “A” shaped professionals, “I” shaped professionals and “multilingual managers”, I think that a Data Scientist needs to be a True T-Shaped professional

The data scientists work with huge amounts of

  • Structured and unstructured
  • Enterprise and third party
  • Clean and unclean
  • Reliable and unreliable datasets

They are expected to generate actionable insights from these. They are expected to identify patterns, sieve through heaps of data and make sense. They would need to have skills that were generally not found in a single individual, they need to be truly T-shaped professionals who have deep business understanding, deep technical expertise, collaborative skills to work with multidisciplinary specialists and business acumen to solve a business problem rather than glorify technology
