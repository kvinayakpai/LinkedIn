Recently I got an opportunity to talk about Artificial Intelligence at the BSPIN Bangalore forum. While I have spoken about multiple topics like Data Warehousing, Business Intelligence, Big Data, Data Science & Security Data Science, this was my first time talking about AI in its full spectrum.

 Here are some snippets of what I spoke (recollected from memory)

What is AI 

If we were to visualize a Human Head as the center of intelligence, then it has the following components

  1. Ability to See
  2. Ability to Listen
  3. Ability to Speak
  4. Ability to Understand, Interpret, Think and act
Ability to See

The area of computer vision is not really new, we have been using video surveillance systems for some time, which have the ability to detect people & objects. There is an intelligence layer being added which allows the program to identify people or objects that it is looking for, or identify objects which are present where they ought not be and take appropriate actions in real time.

Ability to identify images and objects within images has also been there for some time. There is an intelligence layer being added to identify the contents of the image and try to correlate it with the human response to the image.

The most difficult part of vision, IMHO, has been text because it not only requires identification (Optical Character Recognition or popularly known as OCR), it also requires some interpretation. Language has embedded in it, the culture of the region where it is being spoken and specialized vocabulary which is associated with the content of the text (legal jargon in a legal document, medical jargon in medical transcription and IT jargon in a IT related text). The whole area of Natural Language Processing (NLP) has been developing around this, however it has been restricted to English to a great extent. Recently I heard a customer from middle east ask if NLP for Arabic was available and if it was as advanced as English.

Ability to Listen/Speak/Write

We are seeing the rise of systems which respond to voice commands. My 73 year old mother talks to Alexa and gets it to play the audio of her choice streamed directly from the internet.

In the business scenario, we are seeing the whole new area of voice enabled services opening up and in my area of interest voice enabled BI is catching up. Natural Language Generation based on context is also an emerging area. A system that not only shows a report, but provides commentary on the contents of the report is already a reality.

Audio to text conversion has become one of the most spoken business needs. With huge amount of audio being captured, especially in customer service scenarios the need to understand the content of the audio and co-relate it to the human context has become important.

I remember seeing a TED video of a speaker speaking in English and that being translated to Chinese in real time in his own voice!

Ability to Understand, Interpret, Think and act

This is my favorite area of AI and something that I have been associated with for last 9 years. We used to call it Statistical Analysis in those days and then started calling it Predictive Analytics and then Data Science and now Machine Learning. At the heart of this is still Mathematics and Statistics which most of the next generation of certified “Data Scientists”, sadly do not understand :(

We have been working on structured data and have been working on identifying correlations between different variables and interpret the influence of one over the others. We have been building recommender systems which understand customer behavior and predict what could be the Next Best Action for the business to keep the customer engaged. We have systems which prescribe to business players, the action that they need to take to stop a loss from happening.

In the beginning we use to work on small sample datasets and take the inferences from that data and apply it to the whole set. This was because the computing systems didn’t have the capacity to work on the complete data sets. Preparing representative data sets was itself a complex and time consuming task. With the advent of Distributed Computing systems like Hadoop, we started to run the algorithms on whole data sets. Due to sporadic nature of resource requirements the systems started to move to cloud. Another interesting twist that came about was that the code that was working for small datasets, stopped working for larger datasets as it failed to scale and code written in R had to be rewritten in Spark, Scala & Python. The whole concept of Scaled Machine Learning started evolving. Then the demand for real-time Analytics led to streaming analytics.

All this was with structured data, but with newer data like video, audio and images discussed earlier, and the availability of computing power to run Neural Networks, there is a whole new rush to co-relate the content from these newer data sources with the structured data sources to derive newer, hitherto unknown (?) insights and use them to impact the top and bottom lines.

The definition of Data Scientists is fast changing and there is a whole lot of focus on API calling Data Engineers being dubbed as Data Scientists. Data Scientists are being asked to pick up skills like programming, Cloud, DevOps and Containers

In the next few years as IoT devices become more wide spread, decision making would move to the edge! There would be real time decisions that will be made at the edge and consolidating the insights from the edge actions and correlating it with the other sources of data to make edge computing better would make this space more interesting.

What is the relevance of AI

I remember reading somewhere that when a particular strain of AI becomes mainstream it ceases to be AI (like calculators, computers and email sorters and now conversational systems). While AI has been there for a long time as captured in this Stanford report, the reason why businesses are interested in it is because of the impact it could have on their top line and bottom lines. I am more interested in it because of the impact it would have on human life, a glimpse of which can be seen in this video about Human Face of Big Data 

What are the Roles

There are 5 key roles that I highlighted during the talk and they were

  1. Business Analyst
  2. Mathematician/Statistician
  3. Data Engineer
  4. Project/Delivery Manager &
  5. Quality Champion

 While I had spoken/written about the first three roles, the last two are going to become more and more relevant in the near future.

 We keep reading that more than 50% of IT projects fail and this is true with any of the new technology projects like Big Data or AI. One of the key reasons is the lack of processes and governance around these. Strong project managers who are technically savvy are the need of the hour and ensuring quality of the AI output is going to be a new world challenge. So these two roles will evolve in the year ahead.

What about the future of/with AI

As we have already discussed, AI is going to be all pervasive from the window sill next to my mother’s bed to the operation rooms in hospitals. As the cost of GPUs (Graph Processing Units) comes down, there will be AI in every phone and every electronic device (It is already there in some) . Sometime last year, I was sitting in a live demo with a noninvasive head gear attached to my head trying to read my thoughts and felt like the monkey in a lab with electrodes attached. In the near future Brain Computer Interfaces are going to become more prevalent and all this data would make AI more stronger. Smart Dust will give Edge AI a whole new meaning as data in motion would start meaning something else. Quantum computing will revolutionize the AI space as we get closer to Transcendence or Singularity.
