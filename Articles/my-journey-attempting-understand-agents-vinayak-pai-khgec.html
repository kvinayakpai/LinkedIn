My journey in attempting to understand Agents

Created on 2025-01-01 14:41

Published on 2025-01-01 16:00

I want to be a continuous learner and try to keep up with the latest technology trends especially in the Data, Analytics & AI space. When Generative AI was introduced to the world in November 2022, I was curious to learn as much as I could in the shortest possible time. As the space was evolving fast, it was important to have an anchor to build my understanding around it.

I had completed “AI for everyone” facilitated by Andrew NG on Coursera in 2019, so I was waiting for “Generative AI for everyone” and completed it in 2023 soon after it was launched. My understanding around agents was anchored around what Andrew spoke and wrote about .

In this post he wrote that

"I had tested this agent multiple times privately, during which it consistently used a web search tool to gather information and wrote up a summary. During the live demo, the web search API unexpectedly returned with a rate limiting error. I thought my demo was about to fail publicly. To my surprise, the agent pivoted deftly to a Wikipedia search tool — which I had forgotten I’d given it — and completed the task using Wikipedia instead of web search. This was an AI Agentic moment of surprise for me. It’s a beautiful thing when you see an agent autonomously do things in ways that you had not anticipated, and succeed as a result!"

It got exciting when in this video he spoke about how GPT 3.5 could be better than GPT 4.0 with Agents

He called out clearly in this article that

“I think AI agentic workflows will drive massive AI progress this year — perhaps even more than the next generation of foundation models. This is an important trend, and I urge everyone who works in AI to pay attention to it.”

With the anchor in place, I started exploring what various thought leaders had to say about it

 Then I came across Indy Sawhney's series on Agents Part 1, Part 2, Part 3, Part 4 & Part 5

Fast forward to today, I share Aishwarya Naresh Reganti’s beginner-friendly guide to anyone wanting to understand agents

The journey has been exciting trying to understand different experiences and opinions and forming my own opinions along the way.
