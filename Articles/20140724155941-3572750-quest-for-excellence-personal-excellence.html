I attended the “Bullet Proof Manager” workshop from Crestcom and went through a video on “Quest For Excellence” by LISA FORD.

 

The following blog is about what went through my mind. I believe I had started on this journey some time back, however it is a journey and not a destination, so the journey continues..

 

What is excellence? She says “Excellence is an outlook and an attitude” – It indeed is. I started this journey few years back with my reply to the standard question “how are you doing” – My answer was “Excellent”. Initially people thought I was lying, however over a period of time they started to expect this from me and on days I absent mindedly say “Good” they question “What happened are you not doing good?” .There are people who have told me that they like the way I respond to “How are you doing” and gain energy from that.

 

Lisa speaks of three areas of excellence, Personal, Service and Leadership Excellence in that order.

 

She speaks of six keys to Personal excellence

 

1. Love Change – I have spoken to a lot of people about this, have read a lot about this, and was preaching a cousin that she should take life as it comes and “flow like water” because life is like that, it constantly keeps changing. I have listened to spiritual discourses which speak of faith in the supreme power and how we should embrace change as it is part of the divine game. I have heard that psychologically “accepting change” is the best way keep good mental health, and yet I found myself fiercely resisting organizational changes. Lisa helped me realize that to “Love Change” was part of personal excellence, hopefully I will remember it for a long time.

 

She also goes on to speak about 4 stages of change. a) to ignore it b) rebellion c) motivation and d) adaptation or acceptance. It reminded me of the struggle that “Dr Pearson” the protagonist in the novel “The Final Diagnosis” by “Arthur Hailey” faces.

(http://www.booksie.com/other/book_review/nacre_aura/final-diagnosis-a-fitting-tribute-to-the-profession-of-saving-lives)

 

This also reminded me of one of my customers who explained the experience he went through when I decided to move back to India from US. I was working in US on a multi-year multi-phase Data Warehouse migration project with this senior data architect (from the customer side) who seemed to have had developed a lot of affection for me. On my last day at the customer location he spoke of what had gone through his mind when I first broke the news that I was going back home. He said “First I thought he was kidding, then I was angry that he was leaving the project at this stage and going back, then I accepted that this was inevitable and then I saw an opportunity in this, I could work with different people who would bring fresh ideas into the project”. I could relate this to the 4 stages of change that Lisa spoke of.

 

I could also relate it to a journey I went through some time back. I heard of an organizational change happening and affecting me through grape vine first, and I chose “to ignore it”. Then when the change was formally announced I rose in “rebellion” and had heated exchanges with my manager. Then I accepted that this was not going to change and started finding ways to “motivate” myself and others. I realized that I had to “accept it first” before I started motivating others to accept this change.

 

While we seem to love change when it comes to technical gadgets (Samsung Galaxy S2-S3-S4-S5-Sx) we do not seem to like it in our lives, especially professional lives. Lisa says one of the major roles of leaders is to accept change and motivate their teams to accept the change. She says to do this, leaders first should “Love Change”

 

2. Take Risk – During my mentoring sessions with people I have told them that the best way to look at life is to think of a worst case scenario and be prepared for it. This has helped me throughout my professional and personal life. Lisa says “Here are three questions to ask yourself so that you can get more comfortable taking risk. When you're facing that risk ask yourself this first question. What's the worst thing that could happen? And then the second question, what's likely to happen? And the third question, what's the best that could happen? Most of us stop with the first question.”

 

I found that I was so far asking only the first question and stopping at that. I realized that asking the remaining two questions prepares us to take more risks.

 

3. Make Mistakes – This is one thing that I have been listening for the last few years of my professional life. Once a manager told me “Go ahead, take risks, make mistakes, make sure you are learning from your mistakes and never repeat them”. So I advise people to go ahead and try, the only one that does not make mistakes is the one who does not try something new

 

4. Learn something every day – This is something that I have been following for the last few years. This is something which has kept me alive through the ups and downs of the professional life. I have been on the constant journey of learning and that is what keeps me motivated. In fact I learnt something few days back from my son. I was thinking of the most cost effective way of moving training materials to eLearning mode. He was working on a school project preparing PPTs and used audio recording feature in that. He was even able to time the animations in the PPT to suit his narration. Learning from unusual sources is something that I have learnt at my workplace. May it be from movies like “Company”, “Tare Zameen Par” and “Kung Fu Panda” or project management tips from a district collector who planned “Kumbh Mela” or an Army Veteran who managed a regiment in the Kargil war.

 

5. Set Goals – I had never liked goals. Few years back I was counseling a confused team member and he told me he was upset because he did not have any goals. Everyone kept telling him that he had to have goals in life and kept asking him what was his goal. He was upset because he did not have goals. I told him that it was not necessary to have goals always, it was enough if he had something like “I want to be a good human being” and “I want to help my fellow people”. I did not sound convincing to myself. Then I told him that I also did not have goals and kept moving the way life took me. I believed that was the best thing to do and told him so. I also gave him the example of “Michael Crichton” the author of the bestselling novel “Jurassic Park”. Crichton had written an autobiography named Travels which is one of his lesser known books.(http://www.markusehrenfried.de/books/michaelcrichton.html). In this book he writes that one day in his early thirties he was on a flight and realized that he had met all the goals that he had set for himself, he had studied at Harvard Medical School, he had written few books which had become bestsellers and there were even movies made based on his books. It was on this flight that he contemplated suicide because he did not have anything to achieve, he had met all his goals – That is when he starts his inner travel and the book is all about his inner and outer travels. I told this confused team member that if he were to set a goal and pursue that, what would he do if he were to achieve all those by thirty, what about life after that. That conversation was always lingering in my mind because while the person was convinced and managed to lead a happy life after that, I was not convinced with my own explanations and that is why I liked Subroto speak about Purpose of life which was beyond goals (http://www.subrotobagchi.mindtree.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/purpose-and-platform.pdf). It is more important to have a purpose in life and goals in my opinion are intermediate in nature and can change from time to time to suit the purpose of life.

 

Lisa says “It's okay to set a large goal, but be certain you break it into ones that are bite size and manageable. If you have small wins created for yourself and for those around you, then everyone loses the motivation and energy to meet the large goals”. I totally agree with her on this one and feel that as long as the goal is broken into smaller manageable chunks it is easier to achieve them. Many a times if you share a vision with your teams, they might not be able to visualize it, you need to break it into simple bits that everyone could understand, nevertheless it is important to keep the big picture in front of them too.

 

Sometime back my Manager got me to write down my vision for the year and I did it not realizing that I was writing down my goals and breaking it into smaller pieces which enabled me to drive it.

 

6. Ask for criticism – This is something I have been practicing for years and found this very useful. While most of the people feel uncomfortable in giving direct feedback, it at least keeps the channel open and lets them know that they could correct us when needed.

(Months after I wrote this blog, I picked the book Travels again and found that the suicide incident was not there, looks like I had imagined that part,  interesting thing, how memory tricks you)
