Leap from Traditional DW/BI to Advanced Analytics - Part1

Created on 2015-05-09 07:25

Published on 2015-05-09 07:26

A colleague has been asking me to write my point of view on this topic for quite some time. Looks like finally his persistence has paid off. I would first share the journey I have taken and then get into prescriptive mode.

 I started my career as an application developer, went on to become a database administrator and got introduced to Data Warehousing. Till I started working on Data Warehousing I could say my business knowledge was very limited. I could relate to the technical architectures and inner pulses within the database, but could not relate to the business in general. My first project in Data Warehousing changed it forever.  After having worked as a Database Administrator, I was asked if I could do some PL/SQL coding, which I considered mundane. Out of sheer respect for the person that asked me to do it and due to my nature to take up newer things, I accepted. I was asked to code complex supply chain KPIs in PL/SQL. The data marts were in place and the business was waiting to reap the benefits of the investment. I started realizing the complexity of the job at hand and also the relevance of it. I was unaware of the visibility it would get me in the customer organization. I had come up with a high? estimate for the coding of KPIs and I was standing in front of the lady responsible for the success of this program. I had to justify my estimates. I remember myself saying this, “you have spent lot of money in consolidating all the sources and cleaning the data. You have invested in creating ETLs and Reports which would help you reap the benefits of the investment. This piece of code that I am writing is very important to the business. The numbers are going to impact business decisions that would be taken. It is not about the lines of code here, it is about the business use cases and testing to ensure that the KPIs are working as desired under all these test cases. I am sure you would want the KPIs to be 100% accurate”. I got to spend the estimated time in developing those KPIs. When I look back I realize that I had touched the raw nerve there.

 All IT investment is to serve business requirements and if you are speaking the business language and you appreciate those numbers as much as the business does, your journey in being an Analyst or Data Scientist has started.

 A year later I was sitting in Taiwan with two or three customers who were discussing in Mandarin about a calculated measure. I had a reporting tool with me and I built the calculated measure as they were talking and showed it to them. They were awestruck.

 If you understand the business language, you cross the language barrier.

A couple of years later I was in Vietnam, standing in front of a room full of business users talking to them about their business processes and their data model. I was there representing the regional IT team of the customer and was explaining the regional business and data models to them. The objective was to do a gap analysis. The very fact that a group of business users were sitting there listening to me, was something that makes me feel excited even today.

 It happened because I was speaking the business language.

The point I am trying to make is, I have worked in several technologies over the years and if there is one thing that has stayed with me constantly and helped me through the journey, it is the appreciation of the customer’s business processes and the ability to speak their business language.

 For a person to make the leap from Traditional DW/BI to Advanced Analytics, the first prerequisite is to understand the business problems and speak the business language.

 Once you understand customer’s business process, the numbers that are flowing through an ETL job or being reported in a cross-tab report cease to be just numbers. They start speaking to you. You will start understanding the data.

 A couple of years later, I was in Belgium working with a team which had Russian, Swedish, French, Turkish and Belgian colleagues. What worked for me there was the ability to understand their business process and understand their data. I remember discussing Warranty analysis with a project manager and understanding how the number of vehicles which are recalled has impact on suppliers who had provided the parts. I remember discussing the analysis of data from a case management tool and how proper analysis of that data could help us understanding the productivity of the employees.

 For a person to make the leap from Traditional DW/BI to Advanced Analytics, the second prerequisite is to understand the business data and the story they tell about the business processes.

 Now you would say, so what is great in this? A person working in Traditional DW/BI would also know this. That is precisely the point. For a person to move from DW/BI to Analytics, the most important thing is that he should have understood what he/she has been doing over the years. I remember a story that Subroto Bagchi would narrate “Two men were laying bricks. A passer-by asked the first one: “What are you doing?” He said he was laying bricks. Then he asked the second one. That man said: “I am building a temple.”.

 http://subrotobagchi.mindtree.com/building-respect-brick-by-brick/

 Many a times people ask me, should I focus on one ETL tool or One Reporting tool or should I learn more than one tool. Is SQL important, Is it important to learn Data Modeling? and so on and so forth

 My answer to them is 10 years hence you will be expected to

 Interact with customers and understand their expectations

  • Translate the business understanding into the logical data model
  • Be able to communicate confidently with all stake holders
  • Be able to understand and appreciate complex business processes
  • Be able to explain your understanding of businesses process in simple terms to team members
  • Understand the expectations of the customers and build strong working relationships with them
  • Be able to work with people and guide them on their career growth

 Then I ask them, does any of this depend on which tool you work on? The answer clearly is NO. All this depends on how well you understand what you are doing, what are the business problems, what are the business processes and how good are you at stakeholder (customer & internal) management. It all depends on your communication skills, your ability to understand and speak business language, your ability to understand the business processes and the business data, ability to interpret business KPIs and their impact on the business.

 Hope it is now getting clearer that for a person to make the leap from Traditional DW/BI to Advanced Analytics, the third prerequisite is to be able to understand the big picture, understand the temple than worry about the laying the bricks.
