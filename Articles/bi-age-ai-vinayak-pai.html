BI in the age of AI

Created on 2019-10-12 10:08

Published on 2019-10-12 11:00

Summary

===================

While there is lot of hype around AI, the definitions and the business value from them are still being explored. While it is a fancy term for company and individual profiles, the real business value is picking up and yet to be considered significant.

BI on the other hand has served the industry for decades in delivering value and continues to add value even today. Pretty soon organizations would look at money being saved through BI going to fund more of AI. This is an area which is worth investing in for future as post the hype, when real work starts, BI is what matters (As can be seen from the hype around big data today dying down and concern for value out of the big data is being discussed, which is BI on Big Data)

Details

=======

I had an opportunity to present two different lectures in the past month, one on AI to a group of Professors, lecturers and research students, and another on BI to a group of post graduate students on BI.

 

The contrast was that the AI lecture had interesting discussions around what is the definition of AI and the BI lecture had interesting discussions on how we should move away from tools and focus on understanding data and seeing trends. The professor who invited me for the BI lecture told me that he wanted his students to be experts in data analysis, develop an eye for data and tools were secondary.

 

One of the participants in the AI lecture was not convinced with my definition of AI. For him AI was exotic and not something as mundane as the examples I gave.

 

To understand his predicament, let me share the two stories I shared with the group

 

Make AI Pervasive to Deliver Personalized Sales Assortment Recommendations

Using Data Science and Engineering to Build a Price Elasticity Estimator

 

Both are examples of how AI and ML have helped businesses see value and recognized by ISG, A leading global technology research and advisory firm.

 

While I was speaking about the business value of AI, he was busy with the definition of AI and why anything as mundane as the algorithms mentioned in the above stories are not AI. For him, some of the more hot topics like Knowledge Graphs would be AI. Interesting point to note is that as per  Gartner “Hype Cycle for Artificial Intelligence”, knowledge graphs have a market penetration of 1% to 5% of target audience, on the other hand the same report states that Predictive Analytics, has a market penetration of 20% to 50% target audience.

 

This is where my gripe with most of the AI enthusiasts is. They get excited with latest terminologies and get carried away, without actually thinking of business value. We in the industry think of business value first and see how it can impact our customers.

 

The recent Forbes article, What's New In Gartner's Hype Cycle For AI, 2019, discusses 8 new technologies included in this year’s hype cycle namely

  1. AI Cloud Services
  2. AutoML
  3. Augmented Intelligence
  4. Explainable AI
  5. Edge AI
  6. Reinforcement Learning
  7. Quantum Computing and
  8. AI Marketplaces


Interestingly these get discussed often in corporate circles, especially AutoML, Explainable AI and Edge AI, however the work on these is still in PoC (Proof of Concept) stages and no real system in production.

 

While last years Hype Cycle spoke of “Only 4% of CIOs worldwide report they have AI projects in production”, this years hype cycle, says this number has gone up to 14%. The same report also says “This year’s Hype Cycle features many new technologies, but few have a value or purpose that is fully understood”.

 

So to conclude on the AI part of the story, while AI is hot and more businesses are seeing value, it has still got a long way to reach where BI is, in terms of business value.

 

Earlier this month, Gartner released another hype cycle, Hype Cycle for Analytics and Business Intelligence, 2019. This article speaks of Five Major Trends Shaping the Evolution of Analytics and Business Intelligence namely,

  1. Augmented Analytics
  2. Digital Culture
  3. Relationship Analytics
  4. Decision Intelligence
  5. Operationalizing and Scaling

I believe these points touch what is most relevant for businesses today. I will try to substantiate that with one example.

 

As part of the report, Gartner predicts that, by 2020, citizen data scientists will surpass data scientists in the amount of advanced analysis they produce, largely due to the automation of data science tasks. I had written in 2015 about Citizen Data Scientists and stated, You think you can't be a Data Scientist - be a Citizen Data Scientist.

I think Citizen Data Scientists are the people who are enabling BI from AI.

 

In my mind there are 4 types of Data Scientists

  1.  API Data Scientists ( or AI Engineers) – Large cloud organizations like GCP, Azure and AWS have lots of AI packages which can be invoked as APIs to perform AI tasks like Image recognition, NLP and develop Chatbots. The group of people who can use these APIs to work on AI are AI Engineers or API Data Scientists. They will fit the purpose as long as the packages deliver what is promised
  2. Programmer Data Scientists (or ML Engineers) – These are the people who can prepare the data for ML algorithms to work on, can even program the ML Algorithms and build ML pipelines to deploy the ML Models. These people can code and make the life of “Core Data Scientists” better
  3. Core Data Scientists – These are people who come from statistical, mathematical or econometrics kind of educational qualifications. These are people who can think in terms of formulas and equations, the people who think what will be my numerator or denominator when they hear a problem statement. They are the people who can get into the core of the problem and troubleshoot why an algorithm which was giving accuracy of 89% suddenly dipped to 78%
  4. Citizen Data Scientists – These are typically IT savvy business people, who can manage to do the work of the first two groups to some extent. Since most of Data Science can be done without using complex algorithms, they succeed on the basis of their business and data knowledge

 As can be seen from the classification, when Gartner thinks of Citizen Data Scientists, I believe they are thinking of groups, 1,2 & 4.

 In the same article, Gartner predicts that, by 2023, 60% of organizations with more than 20 data scientists will require a professional code of conduct incorporating ethical use of D&A. This is a topic which has now started to become more and more relevant especially with regulations around Data Privacy like GDPR and CCPA.

 To summarize BI was, is and will be relevant. The discussions that happen around AI today are more about possibilities where as the discussions around BI are more mature, how to derive value out of the analysis that we are doing. AI today is more closer to technology where as BI is closer to business.
