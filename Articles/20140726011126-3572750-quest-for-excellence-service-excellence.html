I attended the “Bullet Proof Manager” workshop from Crestcom and went through a video on “Quest For Excellence” by LISA FORD.

 

The following blog is about what went through my mind. I believe I had started on this journey some time back, however it is a journey and not a destination, so the journey continues..

 

Lisa explains the second area which is Service Excellence. She speaks of the following steps

 

A. Service excellence means inspiring people to be their best each and every day – I started thinking about the people who had inspired me and the list I came up within few seconds was a long one. One look at that list and I realized that all these people were leaders in their own way. One of my past managers told me that a leader has to be an inspiration to his people. He spoke of “Customer Leadership”, “People Leadership” and “Thought Leadership”. On Customer Leadership he gave an example of a senior leader who had such a command over customers that they would eat out of her hand (he meant they would go with what she suggests). On the people leadership front he told that if someone were to speak to the leader they should go back inspired to do more and make an impact. On Thought Leadership he said a leader should be able to look beyond the curve and inspire teams to work on keeping their saw sharpened so that they are ready when they reach the curve or the change strikes. I started following this and realized that it was indeed true. I also realized that to inspire others one need not be a leader, few years back a junior team member told me that he was inspired by another team member because of his “never say die” attitude, however it is extremely important for a leader to be able to inspire people. Today my constant attempt is to ensure that people who speak to me go back inspired to do something more. When this happens that is an “aha” moment for me. Few months back I met a bunch of campus recruits as part of the “Meet the Leader” program and I spent an hour telling them about me and answering their questions. I asked them to read a book called “Who Moved My Cheese” by Dr. Spencer Johnson (http://www.flipkart.com/moved-my-cheese-0091816971/p/itmczyzsh3yhttqn) . One of them read it and later wrote to me that the session and the book changed his life. It was an “aha” moment for me. The feeling of inspiring people, making an impact on other people’s lives is what I believe is the purpose of my life. Subroto had said (http://www.subrotobagchi.mindtree.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/purpose-and-platform.pdf) “It is our purpose that determines the impact we make over time”. He also had said that farther the impact the better the feeling. So if people inspired by us inspire others then larger is the impact that we have created. That is the feeling of “Service Excellence”

 

B. Be a bureaucracy buster – We see bureaucracy everywhere and it is present in corporate organizations also. While some bureaucracy is good there are sometimes where bureaucracy kills innovation. One of the stories I tell people is about the story of monkeys and ice water experiment. (http://wiki.answers.com/Q/Did_the_monkey_banana_and_water_spray_experiment_ever_take_place) It takes a lot of courage to be a bureaucracy buster, If we try to change a policy or speak against it, we will meet the fate of the new monkeys in this experiment. I have seen that a way to overcome a process which is not functioning is to capture data about why a process is not right, what are the ways of fixing it, and then try to change it. Few years back when I moved to South Carolina for a project, I was told that my pay would be 20% lesser than my colleagues in New Jersey because of COLA (Cost of Living Allowance). I read about it and found that there was indeed a difference in cost of living between South Carolina and New Jersey, however I did not stop at that, I started collecting data over a period of time and proved that the difference in cost of living was not as high as 20%. The company accepted it and made changes to it, the difference was brought down to 17.5% and then to 15% (Thanks to a proactive HR person). So the message is that it is possible to be a bureaucracy buster, all we have to do is collect data and present it to the relevant people.

 

C. Keep asking what’s new and what next – This is something I have tried and it works. Few years back I met a senior colleague in the corridor and I asked him what’s new and exciting and he told me about “Product Cannibalization” which made me enter into the world of “Analytics” and helped me stay ahead of the curve. It is extremely important to keep asking this question and search for answers, otherwise we might be like the “Dr. Pearson” in the novel “The Final Diagnosis”
