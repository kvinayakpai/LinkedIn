Security Data Science – Fraud and Crime

Created on 2016-07-09 14:04

Published on 2016-07-09 14:10

In my previous blog I had written about why Security Data Science is relevant in today’s context. An interesting session that I attended was about Fraud and Crime in the cyberspace.

Fraud was defined as somebody set out to steal money. Chris McAuley, Director, Fraud and Financial Crimes Unit at SAS explained the persona of a Fraudster as a Man in the age group of 40-50, working for over 10 years for the same employer, playing a middle-senior management role and who has insider knowledge of all internal systems. He said that Fraud is not an instinctive act, but a well thought out planned activity. As a person who has been analyzing fraud for over decades, he says Fraudsters use a pattern, a set of steps and in a way are predictable. While the average planning period of a crime is 26 months, he says the gestation period could be even 5-10 years at times.

Parag Deodhar who is the CISO (Chief Information Security Officer) at AXA group who has been talking about Fraud and Cybercrime at various forums highlighted that organizations should maintain access records of employees, physical movement records & VPN logs to be able to understand standard behavior patterns so that over time they can detect and react in time. He spoke of the POLE model (Person, Object, Location, Event) used in analyzing security data. He highlighted that the biggest challenge is Data Quality and that could be a hindrance in effective decision making. What he said about Dark Web is alarming. “Dark Web is collaborating, if we are not then we are going to lose”

When I did some more reading on Dark Web, it scared me. Have provided a Quora link below for those interested in knowing more.

When I went through Parag’s LinkedIn profile, where he has shared some past presentations, it made me understand the nature of Fraud and Crime in the cyberspace.

The person moderating this discussion Sanjay Sahay is the Additional Director General of Police, Police Computer Wing, Karnataka Police. He said”97% of Fortune 500 companies have been hacked”.

That got me reading more and below is what I found. On one side digital transformation is giving a new high to businesses and on the other side, cybercrime is looming large. I learnt about Cyber Extortion and the TalkTalk data breach. What was more alarming was that cyber criminals accessing biometric data and using it for fraudulent authentication. 

By the end of this session, the role of Security Data Science and that of a Data Scientist in the Data Security space got clearer for me. As more and more organizations go digital, and there is more business data in the cyberspace, the chances of fraudsters getting hold of that data and use it for Cyber Extortion becomes higher. So in this era of digitization, data security becomes more important and gets the attention of businesses and for the Indian IT Industry alone the market for Data Security is $35 Billion by 2020. All this makes Security Data Science a lucrative career option.
