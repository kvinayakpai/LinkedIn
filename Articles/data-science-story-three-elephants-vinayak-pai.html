Data Science and the story of three elephants

Created on 2017-01-14 17:42

Published on 2017-01-14 17:56

The first Elephant that comes to mind for many of us in Data Science world is the elephant associated with big data, Hadoop. So recently when I was reading this blog from Soumendra Mohanty, I was amused to read the comment from one of the CAOs “We can’t do analytics as we do not have Big Data!”. That is the sad story of the Analytics / Data Science world. The focus is on all the wrong things. Today when people speak of Data Science, they speak of either data (big or small) or Science (Statistics, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, neural networks and Artificial Intelligence) but not on the invisible power behind it which has brought it out to lime light, the real thing, business value. So unless Data Science is used to get value out of the business data, it is meaningless.

The second Elephant is, the elephant in the living room. As per Wikipedia it is “an English metaphorical idiom for an obvious truth that is going unaddressed. The idiomatic expression also applies to an obvious problem or risk no one wants to discuss, or a condition of groupthink no one wants to challenge.”

Image Credit - https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3b/The_Elephant_in_the_Room_Banksy-Barely_legal-2006.jpg

Fifteen years back I had worked on implementing a Data Warehouse solution for an industry major and we were trying to solve the data quality problems, defining data governance mechanism and setting up a Master Data Management solution. Last year when I revisited the same organization I realized the problems were pretty much alive and the only thing that had changed was software being used to solve the problems.

Few months back I was demoing one of our solutions to a customer and realized that the customer was interested in one small box in the flow titled “Data Cleaning” and she wanted to know if our solution would automatically identify the data quality problems and fix them. I found myself saying, “contrary to what software vendors try to tell you, there is no tool which will automatically fix your data quality problems”. Businesses need to define processes and change ways of working to have cleaner data in the ecosystem.

As the neurons fired and linked these two instances, suddenly I could see the “Elephant in the living room” called change. In both examples one thing that businesses didn’t want to do was “change”. Businesses had to change their processes so that the data was cleaned and then it was maintained clean. So year after year, software vendors have been selling software which promises to solve the problem and businesses have been ignoring the elephant in the living room.

So if it is big data or small data, Business Intelligence or Data Science, these will not be successful unless the focus is on getting clean data and if required get the businesses to change so that data at the sources is clean.

The third elephant is the one associated with the blind men. 

Image Credit - https://themelkong.wordpress.com/2010/08/31/first-lesson-of-sophomore-year/

It is a story of a group of blind men (or men in the dark) who touch an elephant to learn what it is like. Each one feels a different part, but only one part, such as the side or the tusk. They then compare notes and learn that they are in complete disagreement. Today the story of people who go to explain data science is like the blind men.

  • The Academicians and statisticians feel that the only way of becoming a Data Scientist is through years of research in Mathematics and Statistics, getting a doctorate and researching to get better algorithms which give more accurate results
  • The technical educational institutions repackage their mathematics course as Analytics and Data Science as they feel that is the right thing to do
  • The business educational institutions repackage their courses to say that Data Science course is incomplete without industry coordination, of course they are more closely associated with businesses
  • The IT companies repackage software engineering solutions to solve Data Science problems
  • Armchair philosophers like me say that the real power behind Data Science is Business Value, so unless Data Science is used to solve business value, it is an academician’s pipe dream

So we are all like the blind men who compare notes and feel the others are wrong.

So in summary the three elephants tell me that

  1. Data Science is not about big data but about business value
  2. Data Science will not be successful unless businesses see the elephant in the living room eye-to-eye, change
  3. Data Science is an evolving field and each one could be right and could be wrong, only time can tell who is right and who is not
